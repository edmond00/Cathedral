# A Treatise on the Organs and Their Functions

*A description of the organs proper to the two anatomies, that of Man and that of the Beast, with the physiological office of each, the faculty it lends the frame, and the disciplines that draw upon it. The two frames share one general plan; the points of divergence are noted where they arise.*

*Each organ serves in two regards: it conditions some faculty of the living frame, and it is the seat upon which certain arts depend. The disciplines named are indicative, not exhaustive, being a guide to the character of the organ rather than a full register.*

---

## I. Organs Common to Both Anatomies

### The Encephalon

The cephalic mass, lodged within the cranium, comprising the organs of the intellective and sensitive faculties.

**Cerebrum.** The organ of the understanding, in which perception is reasoned upon and knowledge retained. It is the seat of the reasoning disciplines: the sciences and letters, the reckoning of number and structure, and the schemes of the deliberate mind.

**Cerebellum.** The organ governing the co-ordination of practised movement, and the assimilation of new dexterity under duress. It is the seat of the disciplines of precision and trained technique, whether of the exacting crafts or of the studied forms of combat.

**Hippocampus.** The organ that first registers the impressions conveyed inward by the senses. It is the seat of the receptive and imaginative faculties: the vivid recollection of experience, the composing arts, and openness to what is newly met.

**Anamnesis.** The organ of recollection, governing the persistence and recall of what is learned. It is the seat of the disciplines that rest upon a deep and durable store: accumulated lore, long-remembered technique, and the recollections of a life.

**Pineal Gland.** The organ of consciousness and inward regard, held to be the seat of self-awareness, by which the subject examines its own mind, and of the general readiness by which any discipline is mastered. It is the seat of the contemplative and intuitive arts: meditation, the judgement of beauty, and the presentiments of insight. Of modest development in Man, and lesser still in the Beast.

*The encephalon entire determines the capacity of the attentive faculty, the sum of impressions the subject may hold under consideration at once.*

### The Trunk

The great central cavity, containing the vital and sanguific organs.

**Backbone.** The vertebral column, the axial support upon which the frame is articulated; upon its soundness depend the subject's vigour, endurance, and steadiness under burden. It is the seat of the disciplines of sustained labour and bearing: of load and haulage, and of patient endurance.

**Heart.** The central vital organ and index of the constitution's warmth, its span of years, and its capacity for attachment; the wellspring of ardour and courage. It is the seat of the affective disciplines: the bonds of fellowship, the moving of sentiment, and the passions of desire.

**Viscera.** The abdominal viscera collectively, in which the constitution's temper and fortitude reside, steadying the frame under duress. They are the seat of the disciplines of hardihood: the ferocity of the fray, the endurance of privation, and the coarse and bloody labours.

**Hepar** (*the Liver*). Of the four sanguific organs, that of the sanguine temperament; in health it elaborates chiefly the sanguine humor, inclining to the bilious as it is impaired, and disposing the subject's complexion accordingly.

**Spleen.** Of the four sanguific organs, that of the melancholic temperament; it elaborates the humors as its fellows do, yet withholds the atrabilious humor from ordinary circulation, which grave and particular occasions alone provoke.

**Paunch** (*the Stomach*). Of the four sanguific organs, that of digestion; it renders the humors according to its condition, chiefly the sanguine when sound, the bilious when disordered.

**Pulmones** (*the Lungs*). Of the four sanguific organs, that of respiration; it imparts the humors to the circulation as its fellows do, and lends the wind that sustains prolonged exertion.

**Genitories.** The generative organ, determinant of the sex and seat of the virile vigour, of no further office to the frame. The most rudimentary of the organs. *(Absent in the Beast.)*

*The trunk entire constitutes the vessel of life, the sum of injury the body may sustain before dissolution.*

## II. Organs Proper to Man

### The Visage

The organs of the countenance and of the special senses seated therein.

**Eyes.** The organs of vision, mediating perception at a distance. They are the seat of the disciplines that rest upon the trained eye: observation and scrutiny, aim and marksmanship, the reading of terrain and structure, and the exactitude of visual craft.

**Ears.** The organs of audition, and of the subject's promptitude in answering what occurs about it. They are the seat of the disciplines of attentive hearing: music, vigilance, and the finer attunements of social exchange.

**Nose.** The olfactory organ, the medium of caution and discernment. It is the seat of the disciplines that turn upon scent: the lore of herbs and fungi, brewing and fermentation, and the tracking of what cannot be seen.

**Teeth.** At once masticatory apparatus and armament, reducing the harder aliment for ingestion and delivering the rending bite in conflict. A modest organ, little called upon by the trained disciplines.

**Tongue.** The organ of articulate speech, and the measure of the subject's fluency. It is the seat of the verbal disciplines entire: eloquence and persuasion, language and lore, and the arts of song, story, and company.

*The visage entire governs the comeliness of the countenance, the favour it procures upon a first acquaintance.*

### The Upper Limbs

**Arms.** The organs of gross force: the power of the thrust, the grapple, and the heavy blow. They are the seat of the disciplines of strength: laborious toil, the wielding of weighty implements and arms, and work that taxes the whole frame.

**Hands.** The organs of manual dexterity, by which any implement is turned to use. They are the seat of the broadest family of practical disciplines: the crafts and trades, fine manipulation, and every art of the deft or cunning hand.

### The Lower Limbs

**Legs.** The organs of locomotion, mediating the subject's celerity. They are the seat of the disciplines of movement: running and springing, agility, and the pursuits and evasions of the body in motion.

**Feet.** The organs of stance and endurance in travel, bearing the subject over distance and holding it sure upon uncertain ground. They are the seat of the disciplines of quiet movement and the long road: stealth, wayfaring, and sure footing.

*The lower limbs entire constitute the natural defence, that which of itself deflects an unarmed blow.*

## III. Organs Proper to the Beast

*The Beast conforms to the general plan of Man, but is destitute of the genitories, the hands, feet, arms, and legs of Man, the nose, and the teeth, possessing in their stead the endowments of the wild. Its encephalon comprises the same organs, though of lesser development.*

### The Muzzle

*Comprising the Eyes, the Ears, and the Tongue after the common manner, together with:*

**Snout.** The olfactory organ of the Beast, of far greater development than the nose of Man, the sense by which prey is tracked and the predator detected; the seat of its disciplines of scent and pursuit.

**Fangs.** The principal armament of the Beast, in the place of the teeth of Man: the instrument of the killing bite, its power varying with the species. The seat of the predatory arts of the maw.

### The Limbs

**Legs.** The locomotor organs of the Beast, propelling it at a pace no biped may maintain; the seat of the disciplines of the chase.

**Claws.** The lacerating armament of the paw; the seat of the rending arts of the stroke.
