# Cosmology

What is written here is the cosmology **as the Ten Chapters tell it**, which is the elf's account as
understood by Hesperine and Oudin Ban around −903 FC, plus what actually lies behind it where that
differs. The chapters are not a lie; they are a translation made by people who needed a religion and
had a prisoner who needed to be understood.

---

## The Principia

A **principium** (plural **principia**) is not a god in the sense of a person who wants things. It
is a *necessity that has become a presence*: the answer to a question, which exists because the
question was asked. The elf's word for them, *prin-thiel*, means roughly "what had to be so".

The core doctrine, and the thing every Principist child learns first:

> **A principium asks a question of another principium, and the answer is a new principium.**

Two principia were not answers. **Silance**, because nothing precedes nothing. **Adswaru**, because
someone has to ask. The chapters do not say where Adswaru came from, and the churches have fought
over that silence for three thousand rounds.

**Who speaks.** In every chapter the roles are the same. One principium **asks**, and another is
**asked**. The one asked **answers**, and the answer does not stay a sentence: it becomes the new
principium, which from then on *is* that answer and speaks it in its own voice. That is why the
answers of Chapters 5 and 6 are in the first person: "I will watch...", "I will create...". The
voice is the newborn principium's, and the answer came from the one asked.

| chapter | asks | is asked | the answer, which becomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | none | none | (Silance is; the page is blank) |
| 1 | none | none | (Adswaru is; it asks) |
| 2 | Adswaru | Silance | **Repance** |
| 3 | Adswaru | Repance | **Rede** |
| 4 | Adswaru | Repance | **Rhema** |
| 5 | Adswaru | Repance | **Rhesis** |
| 6 | Adswaru | Repance | **Qoth** |
| 7 | Qoth | Rede | the **Random Forest** (a world, not a principium) |
| 8 | Qoth | Rhema | the **Cosmic Orgy** |
| 9 | Qoth | Rhesis | the **Eternal Temple** |
| 10 | Qoth | Adswaru | the **Cosmic Sea** |

Silance answers only once, and it answers the way nothing can: by being the absence against which
the question lands. The commentaries say Repance is "Silance's answer spoken by Adswaru's question".
After Chapter 2 every question of Adswaru's goes to Repance. What exists can only be understood
through its disappearing.

### The seven

**Silance**, *the first principium, of nothing.*
- Chapter 0, which has a title and no text. In every copy of the Chapters the page is left blank, and
  copyists who wrote on it were punished.
- Symbol: the empty page, an unmarked square of vellum. On buildings, a blank niche.
- Epithets: *the Unsaid, the Clean Page, the Mother of Question.*

**Adswaru**, *the second principium, of reason and the axioms of existence.*
- Chapter 1. The Asker. It asks every question of Chapters 2 to 6, and every principium after it is
  born of those questions. It answers only once, in Chapter 10, when Qoth brings it the ashes, the
  tears and the sand. That single answer ordered the world we live in.
- Symbol: the hook (a question-mark without its dot, or a shepherd's crook).
- Epithets: *the Asker, the Crook, the First Why.*

**Repance**, *the third principium, of time and destruction.*
- Chapter 2. **Adswaru asks Silance:** *"Why is there something rather than nothing?"* **Silance
  answers**, and the answer becomes Repance: *"What exists will disappear into nothing over time."*
- It is Repance, not Qoth, who is present in every chapter from 2 to 10: it is asked every question
  of Chapters 3 to 6, and it undoes each of Qoth's worlds.
- Symbol: the hourglass, or a handful of ash.
- Epithets: *the Undoer, the Patient, the One Who Comes Back.*

**Rede**, *the fourth principium, of randomness and chaos.*
- Chapter 3. **Adswaru asks Repance:** *"If what exists always disappears, why must it exist at
  all?"* **Repance answers**, and the answer becomes Rede: destruction is not determined by the nature
  of the thing destroyed. It comes by chance, so the thing must exist for chance to find it.
- Symbol: three knucklebones.
- Epithets: *the Scatterer, the Throw.* Invoked by gamblers, sailors and soldiers.

**Rhema**, *the fifth principium, of emotions and sensations.*
- Chapter 4. **Adswaru asks Repance:** *"Why does disappearance itself exist, and not only the thing
  disappearing?"* **Repance answers**, and the answer becomes Rhema: because when things disappear,
  something is felt: pain or relief, regret or contentment.
- Symbol: a drop (a tear, or a drop of blood; which one depends on the church).
- Epithets: *the Feeling, the Wet-Eyed.*

**Rhesis**, *the sixth principium, of observation and judgment.*
- Chapter 5. **Adswaru asks Repance:** *"Who will feel what when something disappears?"* **Repance
  answers**, and the answer becomes Rhesis, who speaks it in its own voice: *"I will watch every
  destruction and judge which feeling befits which thing, so that feeling stays coherent with what
  happens."*
- Symbol: the open eye, or the balance.
- Epithets: *the Watcher, the Judge, the Measure.* Patron of magistrates, physicians and critics.

**Qoth**, *the seventh principium, of reality.*
- Chapter 6. **Adswaru asks Repance:** *"What will disappear?"* **Repance answers**, and the answer
  becomes Qoth, who speaks it in its own voice: *"I will create what will be destroyed."*
- The last principium. From Qoth onward, questions produce **worlds**, not principia.
- Symbol: the open hand, palm up, or the seed.
- Epithets: *the Maker, the Gardener* (after the Eleventh Chapter), *the Hand.*

### Reading the genealogy

The chapters are a chain of questions. Churches differ on whether the chain is **history** (these
things happened in an order), **logic** (these things imply one another and have no order), or
**psychology** (they are the thoughts of one mind; see Figurative Qothism in `07_religions.md`).
The elf, as far as the Ban understood it, meant something like the second.

## The four worlds

From Chapter 7 on, Qoth creates, each time taking counsel of one principium. Repance destroys each
creation, not out of malice, since it is simply what Repance *is*. What remains is kept. Nothing in
the cosmology is ever thrown away.

### World 1: the Random Forest (Chapter 7)

> Qoth asked Rede: *"What should I create?"* "An endless forest, where trees, plants and mushrooms
> grow chaotically in all directions."

A forest with no ground and no sky, growing in every direction at once: roots becoming branches,
fungus threading through heartwood, flowers opening inside trunks. Its people were the **wood elves**,
living plants in human shape.

Repance saw it and **burnt it**. The fire lasted, the chapter says, "as long as the forest had lasted".
What remained were **ashes and embers**. The ashes did not settle and the embers did not go out.

### World 2: the Cosmic Orgy (Chapter 8)

> Qoth asked Rhema: *"What should I create?"* "A limitless feast, with unlimited dishes and guests
> of infinite beauty."

A banquet without walls or end: tables to every horizon, dishes that refilled, wine that never
soured, and guests, the **blood elves**, who were beauty itself, lying together without shame or
fatigue.

Repance saw it, and it **rotted**. The food spoiled on the plates. The guests grew old and ugly for the
first time and wept at what they had become. They wept until their tears were a sea, and then they
slept in it. What remained were **tears**.

### World 3: the Eternal Temple (Chapter 9)

> Qoth asked Rhesis: *"What should I create?"* "A majestic temple of marble and gold, where I could
> sit enthroned and judge all things."

A temple where Qoth, the maker, sat as judge, served by the **white elves**, who worshipped Rhesis and
kept its rites. The chapter lists its halls: the Hall of Measures, the Hall of Verdicts, the Hall of
the Unjudged, and others.

Repance saw it and **waited**. With time the walls wore down to **sand**. This is the only world Repance
destroys by doing nothing, and the Chapters' commentators have made much of that.

### World 4: the Cosmic Sea (Chapter 10)

> Qoth showed the ashes, the tears and the sand to Adswaru: *"What should I do with this?"* Adswaru
> said: "The ashes should go up, the sand should go down, and the tears in the middle."

This is the **present world**, the only one of the four that has not been destroyed:

- **Above**, the **ash-sky**: an endless drift of the forest's ashes, lit by its **embers**, which float
  in it like stars and give all the light there is.
- **In the middle**, the **cosmic sea**: the tears of the guests, endless and salt.
- **Below**, the **sand-floor**: the ground-down temple, strewn with what did not wear away.

Chapter 10 ends there. Qoth made no fifth world. Or the Chapters record none; the Eleventh Chapter,
the forgery, claims to supply the sequel.

**The eleven chapters**: Chapter 0 (Silance) to Chapter 10 (the Sea) makes eleven texts. The
**11-day week** of the Principist calendar gives one day to each, and the silent day is Chapter 0's.

## The elves

**The elves are the people of the first three worlds, and they are immortal.** They do not age out of
existence; they persist through the destruction of their worlds, changed by it. They are *remnants*,
like the ashes, tears and sand.

| kind | world | appearance | where they are now |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wood elves** | Random Forest | living humanoid plants, skin like thin bark, hair like root-thread or moss | none known unburnt. **[proposal]** A few are said to have survived as seeds in the embers. |
| **Ash elves** | the burnt forest | wood elves after the fire; skin like coal, cracked, glowing faintly in the cracks | adrift in the ash-sky, among the embers |
| **Blood elves** | Cosmic Orgy | the most human-looking: skin like human skin, perfect proportions, no body hair, long-lobed ears, eyes with no visible white | adrift and unconscious in the cosmic sea |
| **White elves** | Eternal Temple | skin like polished marble, cold to the touch, pale hair; servitors and worshippers of Rhesis | presumed buried in the sand-floor, among the temple ruins |

### The blood elf of the Ban

The elf held captive for 4,782 rounds (−1203 to 3579), 151 of them by the twins and 4,631 by the
Ban, was a **blood elf**, male, one of the guests.
The Red Brick House called him **the Guest**, its servants **the Well**, and Beatildists **the Sleeper**.
Hesperine's codex gives his own name as **Semmerel**, which in his tongue means something like "the
seventh at the sweet table", since guests of the orgy were named for their places at the feast.

- His blood heals disease and wounds, restores youth, and prolongs life. In excess, or over long
  enough, it **deforms**: it drives growth without bound (see `03_world_science.md`).
- He was lucid for only a few days in a hundred even when the twins held him. The Ban questioned him
  in those windows. From c. 212 FC, after he came close to breaking his chains, the Balnus kept him
  in a permanent stupor with poppy-milk. Away from the drug, he starts to wake over a few days, as
  Beatilda found.
- What he remembers most clearly is the feast, and the moment the food turned. Much of the Ten
  Chapters' vocabulary of loss comes from him.
- **[hidden]** Blood elves are not unconscious because they are asleep. They are unconscious because
  they are *grieving*, and grief in an immortal does not end. The Guest's lucid windows were the
  moments when pain let him think.

## The cosmic sea

The cosmic sea is the only world that exists now. Everything else, every inhabited world, is
**inside** it:

- **Giant jellyfish** are born and live in the cosmic sea. They are the largest living things.
  Imperial observers who saw one from a submarine described a bell "wider than the Inland Seas" and
  trailing threads "longer than a voyage".
- The jellyfish **lay eggs**. Each egg is a world: an **eggmoon**. Pyr, Belune, Varam and every world
  of the empire are eggs of the cosmic jellyfish.
- The eggs float at different depths. High eggs, near the ash-sky, are bright. Low eggs, near the
  sand, are dim. From any world, the other eggs appear as **moons** (if near) or as **stars** (if far),
  among the embers.
- Blood elves drift in the sea, unconscious. Now and then one is drawn into a vortex and falls into a
  world. This has happened at least once, and **[proposal]** perhaps a dozen times in all the worlds'
  history, which would account for the "sea-saint" and "drowned angel" legends found on several
  worlds.

For the anatomy of an egg, the vortices, and how an egg hatches, see `03_world_science.md`.

### Hatching

When an egg hatches, its world ends. The shells split, the seas pour out into the cosmic sea, and a
new jellyfish swims free. It is the only natural end of a world.

The Ten Chapters do not mention hatching at all. The elf either did not know of it or was not asked.
Hatching came into Pyrean thought through **Medusosianism**, from Belune, from the dwarfs, who know
it as the purpose of everything. See `07_religions.md` and `12_secrets_and_mysteries.md`.
