# Secrets and Mysteries

Everything marked **[hidden]** elsewhere is gathered here and explained. Answers the draft did not give
are marked **[proposal]**: each one fits everything else, and each can be changed without disturbing
anything outside its own entry.

No in-world source knows more than a fragment of what follows. Books should **hint**, never state.

---

## Part I: secrets the world lost

### 1. The elf prince was the Baron
The half-elf legend says an elf prince came to the Princess. The "elf prince" was **Ansedor**
himself, a grey old baron who became a young man overnight after drinking the Guest's blood and then
took his own daughter as his wife. The household called him "the stranger" for a while, because he was
unrecognisable. Only the Red Brick House kept the truth, in Hesperine's notes.

### 2. The Closing of the Well (c. −300)
After Merove's ruling, the Ban-Aloid were never told that the baptismal draught came from a living
prisoner. They believed it was "the preserved blood of the half-elf ancestors". So the emperors of Pyr, for
seventeen centuries, did not know what they were. Beatilda was the first Aloid in a thousand rounds to find out.

### 3. Aqilon killed his brother
Verson Ban-Aloid recognised Aos's hand in the Eleventh Chapter, from the letters the two cousins had
exchanged as boys, and resolved to denounce the forgery at the session. Aqilon poisoned him the night
before with a tincture from the Red Brick House's pharmacy. Losing the vote was then turned to profit, since
riots require a grievance. The Awakened Prince, a sincere mystic, had been fed money and crowds by Aos's
agents for two rounds without knowing it.

### 4. Verson II knew his father
The man carried to the gates of Avolor in 1 FC was plainly Aqilon. Verson II had governed for thirteen
rounds and would not give it back to a feverish cripple who had murdered his uncle, a fact Verson II had
been told by his mother Orbanne. He called his father an impostor so that nobody would have to call him a
parricide.

### 5. Grizx never preached sacrifice
Grizx preached **letting things burn**: not rebuilding, not holding on, letting chance take what it takes.
His followers turned it into burning people. Grizx walked into the Pereth one night in 247 and drowned,
and the Children of Rede said he had been "taken up in fire".

### 6. The Burning Council was an accident
The fire of 1004 started in the stove of the lower library, overfed on a cold night by a novice who had
been told to keep the archive dry. Nothing and nobody judged Firaperc. Three churches came of a stove.

### 7. The last warden and the Seagull's price
**Hamelin Moll**, last hereditary warden of the Well's door, loved Beatilda from a distance and let her
and Jhoeland in. **Jhoeland Leneu** betrayed her because the Ticklers' Guild had taken his mother and sister.
He bought two lives with one. The Guild kept its word, and cut out his tongue anyway.

### 8. Bemakor bought the assassination
Akilon XII was killed by Ysmé Varrocq, but the hairpin was paid for by his brother **Bemakor**, through a
Tickler cell and a Red Tribunal intermediary, to open a regency and dispose of the heir. Blaming the Guild was
not wrong; it was incomplete.

### 9. Who knew what about the hatchings
Nobody on the surface of any world understood hatching as a fact. The dwarfs did. After 1703 a few Balnus
physicians and, later, the Far Lodges **suspected** that Varam's hatching was connected with the girl
they had sent there. They were right, and they drew the wrong conclusion (see 11).

## Part II: how the cosmos actually works

### 10. The souls of the dead feed the embryo
This is the Medusosian creed, and it is **literally true**. Every soul on an eggmoon sinks at death into the
embryo, which grows on them and hatches when full. The Eleventh Chapter's Garden does not exist. The
Principist dead of Pyr, seeds in hand, are all in the jellyfish that swam away in 3579.

### 11. Elf blood quickens [proposal]
A soul that has carried elf blood is **richer food**: the Orgy's *more, always more* comes down into the
embryo with it.

- **Pyr** had been fed for 4,600 rounds on the souls of blood-drinkers: every Ban who ever died, and the
  countless buyers of the cordial of Ban. On top of that it was the most populous and most violent world of
  the empire. Its embryo was **gorged** long before 3579. The Ban fattened the god that ate them.
- **Varam** was an old, high, nearly full egg, and a prison-world since c. 1400, where prisoners died in
  numbers. **Beatilda**, baptised with the blood, tortured and starved, died in the ice of Vaur within days of
  arriving. Her death tipped it over. The Beatildists are right that Varam hatched *because of her*. They are
  wrong that it hatched *for* her; nothing chose her. Her soul is in the Varam jellyfish, with every
  prisoner's.

### 12. The red creature and the goblins [proposal]
The "elf of the temple" brought back by the *Patience of Rhesis* was not an elf. It was an **egg-mite**, a
parasite of the cosmic jellyfish, living in the sand-floor on whatever sinks: dead jellyfish, shell-floes,
temple debris. In the lightless deep, egg-mites are **red**.

- An egg-mite in a living egg **seeks the embryo**: it burrows downward, and its presence **quickens**
  hatching. It is how the mites reproduce: when the egg hatches, the mite's brood is scattered with the
  world's seas into the cosmic sea.
- Brought to Pyr, the mite (a gravid female) quickened an embryo that was already gorged. Pyr hatched **within
  days**.
- Its brood drifted in the cosmic sea and was drawn down through the vortex eyes of many worlds. Under the
  ember-light of the outer shells, feeding on surface food, the brood grew **green**. These are the
  **goblins**, first seen c. 70 AH.
- Goblins **tunnel**, and they tunnel **down**, into mines, caves and ruins, toward the hollow layers and the
  embryos. **Wherever goblins thrive, the world's end is nearer.** No one in the world knows this. The dwarfs,
  who have begun to meet goblins in the hollow layers, are finding out.

This gives the post-imperial present its quiet dread without adding any event: the worlds are not only
fallen from an empire; some of them are slowly being brought to term.

### 13. The Guest after the Hatching [proposal]
An elf does not die, and an elf has no soul for an embryo to take. When Pyr hatched, the Guest (Semmerel)
was flung out into the cosmic sea with Pyr's waters, free for the first time in 4,782 rounds, and **awake**,
since the Balnus drug wore off within days. What he did next is unknown. Sightings of "the pale swimmer" on
a dozen worlds after the Hatching may be him, or other blood elves, or nothing.

### 14. The Chapters are true as far as the elf knew
The Ten Chapters are the Guest's account of what came before, as understood by two humans through a
language they only half learned. **The four worlds, the elves and the cosmic sea are real.** The Principia
are how the elves understood the necessity behind them. Whether the Principia are *persons* is a question
the elves could not answer either. The Chapters say nothing of the jellyfish, eggs or hatching because the
guests of the Orgy slept through the whole of the fourth world's history.

## Part III: open questions

Left deliberately unresolved. Books may speculate freely; these files do not decide.

### 15. Where did the first jellyfish come from?
No chapter mentions them. Possibilities any in-world thinker might propose:
- they are **Repance's** instrument in the fourth world: the sea's way of making things so that they can end;
- they are what became of the **Orgy's feast**: appetite given a body;
- they are older than Qoth's worlds, and the chapters are only one creature's memory.

### 16. Where do humans come from?
Principism: Qoth's fourth guests. Medusosianism: the embryo's dreams. Dwarfs: the herd. Nobody knows.

### 17. What happened to Vesifia III? [proposal]
She was alive at the Hatching, twenty-five metres tall, fed by a household of several hundred inside the
unfinished Great Red Pyramid. With Pyr gone no more blood came, and a Balnus off the blood sickens. The
household rationed the last stocks. **[proposal]** She died around 30 AH; the household sealed her chamber,
dispersed, and became the dune-clans who still guard the approaches. The "breathing in the stones" that
pilgrims report is wind in the pyramid's air-shafts. The legend should never be settled on the page.

### 18. Where did Arneviz Geoant go? [proposal]
He sailed in 2871 for a dim point beyond the tables. **[proposal]** He tried to rise to the ash-sky to reach
the embers, which the Far Lore held to be the living remnant of the Random Forest, and his submarine
clogged in the ash-silt. No one will ever find it. The Far Lodges expected him back until the Hatching.

### 19. Are there other elves loose in the worlds?
Possibly. See 13 and `09_peoples_and_creatures.md`.

### 20. Will other worlds hatch?
All of them, eventually. When is the only question, and goblins may be the answer (see 12).
