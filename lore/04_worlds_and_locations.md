# Worlds and Locations

The draft named eleven worlds. The empire at its height knew 299 and held 229; the ones worth naming
are below. Imperial names are given first. Worlds had native names before the Ban came and new
local names after the Ban were gone, and a book may use either.

**Note on the game's sky.** Imperial star-tables (the *Tables of Barnutum*, revised down to c. 3400
FC) list **383 moons** visible from the heart of the empire. Post-imperial star-readers name moons
from old syllables (Armoth, Belarath...), and these names rarely match the imperial ones.

---

## PYR: the Ban world (hatched 3579 FC)

**Symbol "o("**: the Yellow Island to the west, the crescent continent to the east. Painted on
imperial banners, cut into milestones, stamped on coins.

- An eggmoon of middling depth in the cosmic sea. Round of 360 days (the standard).
- Temperate in the south, cold and mountainous in the north.
- One continent, **Vahyr**; one large island, the **Yellow Island** (Vu). Scattered islets.
- **The Inland Seas**: the calm, fresh seas held inside the crescent. Trade water.
- **The Sea of Vu**: the open, rough western ocean between Vu and Vahyr.
- Vortices: the **Southern Throat** (beyond Benepelop's horn) and the **Northern Throat** (beyond Dokur).
- **Nearest moon: Belune**, large and pale. **Brightest: Varam** (until 1703).

Pyr no longer exists. What survives of it is what was carried off-world: records, relics, heirlooms,
names. Many post-imperial places are called "New Pyr", "Little Avolor", "Dokur-over-Sea".

### VAHYR, the crescent

Shaped like a crescent loaf curving east, "(". Its outer (western) coast is cliffs and storms; its inner
(eastern) coast is harbours on the Inland Seas. The **Harrow Range** runs down its spine from Dokur to
Napel, highest in the north.

#### Benepelop, the southern horn

Warm, fertile, rich; olive terraces, vineyards, merchant palaces. The cradle of the Ban and of Principism.

- **Avolor.** Capital of Benepelop, then of the empire for 3,580 rounds. Built on seven hills around a
  harbour on the Inland Seas.
  - **The Clay Hill**, crowned by the **Red Brick House** (−505): a windowless fortress-manor of dark red
    brick, where the elf was kept in the **Well-Room** below ground. Its gatehouse was held by the
    hereditary wardens of House Moll. After 2700 FC the **Pyramid of the Clay Hill**, first of the Red
    Pyramids, rose behind it.
  - **The Old House**: the Ban townhouse where Principism was first taught (c. −880). It became a warren
    of libraries, chapels and cells, and burned on 1004 FC with its whole house. The site was left as a
    walled field of ash, the **Burnt Close**, and Genesives and Figuratives disputed its ownership for
    centuries.
  - **The Salt Palace**, formerly the **Hundred Gardens Palace**. In its **Garden of Lilies** is the
    **Salt Mound**, under which King Jevunavosh was buried alive (601). Imperial residence from 601. The
    **Lily Wing**, Beatilda's residence, burned in 1702.
  - **The House of Thirty-Three**: the Ekklesia's hall, with its thirty-three stone chairs. After the
    Foundation it was the imperial Senate chamber, and later the seat of the Plebeian Tribunal.
  - **The Tower of Salts**: the salt magazine of the old republic, later a prison. The Awakened Prince
    died there (−31), and Aqilon I (2 FC).
  - **The Ropewalk**: the quarter of rope-makers and tanners, where the Garden Riots began and the Quelling
    ended. A penitential procession walked it every Silent Day eve for a thousand rounds.
  - **The Market of the Sowing**, where the Vunic merchants were buried alive in 593.
  - **The Genesive See**: the cathedral of Alos Ban-Goerim, built facing the Burnt Close.
- **Kalom.** Walled trading town north of Avolor. Besieged in −12, plague-struck by Lwrash's corpse
  catapults; its **Plague Pits** outside the north gate were a place of dread for centuries. Birthplace
  of Lady Ermesinde, Hesperine's mother. The lands of the golden knight's house, **Moll**, lay nearby.
- **Ban.** A small hill barony two days inland from Avolor: **Ban Keep**, a squat tower-house, and
  **Hesperine's Tower**, where the elf was first chained and the Ten Chapters were begun. A place of
  pilgrimage under the Aloid emperors ("the Cradle"), then quietly closed by the Balnus. In the
  official legend it is where the elf prince came to the princess.

#### Napel, the river country

Flat, wet, rich in grain. Levees, river-towns, barge-trade.

- **Yr.** Capital of Napel. Taken by Aqilon (−11), besieged (−10 to −9) and burnt with the coalition
  army inside. Rebuilt by Verson II as **New Yr**. The ash layer beneath it was left exposed in one
  place, the **Ash Field**, as a monument.
- **Lusk.** Small market town on the lower river, where Dusher Polosik was priest; see of the Plain
  Church (Principative Qothism). Its **Plain Chapel** has no altar, no image and no bell.
- **The Levee Towns**: Hurn, Saddle, Oxmere, Little Yr. Napelese river-folk, barge-guilds.

#### Penaros, the land of bridges

Hilly, crossed by rivers, famous for its bridges and its schools.

- **Perorigak.** Capital of Penaros. The **Foundling House**, which Grizx burnt (211) and which was
  rebuilt; its orphans take the surname **Perorin**. The **Blind Cells**, lightless dungeons under the
  citadel. The **Figurative See** of Adelim Ban-Goerim.
- **Perostro.** Port city across the river from Perorigak; home of the **IISTG** and its **Guildyards**,
  the greatest shipyards of Pyr, later the submarine arsenal of the empire. The Second Prunil Expedition
  sailed from here in 1703, without its three stowaways.
- **The Pereth.** The broad river between the two cities. The **Rain Crossing** (−8) is remembered at
  the **Rafters' Stair**, a ford-side monument of carved roof-beams.

#### Pebelos, the northern horn

Mountains, snow, pine, iron; clans and hearths. Pebelos was the last free republic to fall.

- **Avenas.** A lake town reached by a long causeway. Here Vhomanor Hask killed Lwrash (−6) in the
  **Battle of Avenas**. A giant's thigh-bone was displayed on the causeway gate until the Balnus took the
  throne and removed it.
- **Sorvas.** A mountain town under the **Sorvan Shoulder**, buried with its defenders by Aqilon's
  sappers (−1). Never excavated except for Vhomanor's body. The avalanche slope, **the Tomb-Slope**, grows
  no trees.
- **Dokur.** Capital of Pebelos, at the northern tip of the crescent. Here the empire was founded (0 FC)
  in the **Hearth-Moot Hall**, and Aqilon's leg was cut off in a merchant's house afterwards called
  **the House of the Leg**, which kept the saw.
- **The Harrow Range.** The spine of Vahyr. Iron mines, clan-holds, the pass-forts of the Mountain War.

### THE YELLOW ISLAND: Kingdom of Vu

Yellow from its sulfur-and-salt crusts and its dry yellow grass. Harsh, windy, sea-facing. Vu was a
kingdom of fishermen, salt-miners and sailors, ruled by the **Tide-Kings** for two thousand rounds.

- **Hanavun.** Royal city, on a bay of the east coast facing Vahyr. The **Yellow Court**, a palace of
  unmortared yellow stone with its **Sitting Hall**, where the court sat in silent meditation each
  morning. Besieged and taken in 600 to 601.
- **Osk.** The salt mines of central Vu: shafts, galleries, and underground chapels of the Stillness
  carved in rock salt. The only salt on Pyr.
- **Lemavosh.** The great harbour of the west coast; the imperial landing of 599.
- **Tavunesh Cove.** A grey shingle cove on the wild west coast, where the **fishing hut** stood.
  Burnt by the black knight in −1052. Vunic fishermen avoid the cove; the story is told in Vu as the
  **Tale of the Two Sisters of Tavunesh**.
- **The Straits of Omavosh.** The narrows between Vu's southern cape and Benepelop's horn, the gate of
  the salt route. Site of Vu's great naval victory (596).

## BELUNE: the nearest moon (a kingdom from 1321)

Seen from Pyr as a large pale moon; the second world of the empire.

- Smaller than Pyr, hilly, volcanic-seeming, with **hot springs** everywhere: steaming valleys, mineral
  terraces, sulfur lakes. Their waters cure black bile corruption.
- Round of 344 days.
- **Natives**: small, independent **tribes** of hunters and herders, Medusosian by faith. Nine tenths
  died in the **Exchange Plagues** (1090 to 1140). The survivors, the **Belunese Remnant**, live in the
  high valleys.
- **The Imperial Thermal Sanatorium (ITS).** Built 1212 around the greatest spring, **the Hundred
  Steams**: a palace-hospital of terraced baths, galleries and gardens. Jelebanne's residence, then the
  royal palace of Belune and the second capital of the Two-Headed Empire. Its **Academy of the Springs**
  (1362) rivalled Avolor's.
- **The Gullet.** A deep cave-mouth in the northern hills, through which **Nemeth Oraun** descended to
  the hollow layer. Sealed by the imperial governors, reopened by Medusosian pilgrims, sealed again.
- **Oggerund.** The dwarf kingdom in Belune's hollow layer, known only from Nemeth Oraun's account.
- **The Causeway of Oranse.** The raised road across the Oranse mud-flats where Bemakor died (2532).
- **The Three Red Pyramids of Belune** (c. 2800 to 3000): at the Sanatorium, at Oranse, and at Kell
  Harbour.
- After the Hatching, Belune briefly styled itself an empire (c. 10 AH) and then broke apart.

## VARAM: the brightest moon (hatched 1703 FC)

- A high egg, near the ash-sky, which is why it shone so bright. Its surface was nearly all **ice**,
  with rock ridges and black lakes under the ice.
- Uninhabited before the empire except by **[proposal]** a few ice-fishing clans on the equatorial
  ridges. The empire used it as a **prison-world** from c. 1400, and prisoners died there in numbers.
- **Vaur**, the ice dungeon: cells cut into a glacier, reached by a single descending tunnel. Beatilda
  was sent there in 1703.
- Hatched a few days after her arrival. Gone from the sky. The Beatildists' holy "place" is an absence.

## PRUNIL (conquered 1705 to 1722)

- A mountainous world of sheer ranges, gorges and high valleys; **salt** and **gold** mines everywhere
  (the rain shell has slumped over whole regions).
- **Small independent mountain cities**, each with its salt-guild and gold-guild, notorious for
  smuggling and for sheltering fugitives from Pyr.
- **Carrow-in-the-Salt**: the largest city, carved into a salt mountain. Besieged 1709.
- **The Gorges**: where Rosena's troops drove the resisting cities' people off the cliffs (1713).
- **Aurimont**: the gold mountain; imperial mint of the Nine Pearls.
- **Rosena's Stair**: the queen's palace, a tower built as tall and thin as she was.

## THE THREE AVORIAS (conquered by Dosh Avory, 1731 to 1771)

Named after their conqueror, whose house governed them for three centuries.

- **Green Avoria**: forests and jungles, rain nearly every day; tree-cities of the natives, the **Canopy
  Folk**. Timber for the IISTG. After the Hatching, jungle reclaimed most imperial works.
- **Blue Avoria**: one great ocean and thousands of islands; a people of navigators and divers. Home of
  **tide-opera** (sung on floating stages) and of its famous courtesans, one of whom killed Akilon XII.
- **Golden Avoria**: dunes, dry salt-pans, rock deserts, oases. Late in the empire, place of exile of
  **Vesifia III** and site of the **Great Red Pyramid** (begun 3420, never finished), the tallest
  structure ever raised by the Ban. Pilgrims and treasure-seekers still go there.

## NEW PYR and AQILONIA (conquered by Violann III, 1780 to 1799)

- **New Pyr**: a temperate world "like Pyr made larger", colonised heavily from Pyr. Its cities copied
  Avolor's plan: seven hills, a Clay Hill, a salt market. After the Hatching it claimed to be Pyr's heir.
- **Aqilonia**: named for the Lame Emperor. Rolling plains and river-deltas; horse-breeding; the
  **Aqilonian masques**, court theatre played in masks of the emperors.

## NEW VARAM (discovered 1811, conquered 1815 to 1829)

- The most distant world of the Nine Pearls, over 900 days from Pyr. Named for the lost Varam because
  it too is icy and bright.
- Native **ice-cantors** whose throat-song carries across glaciers.
- Beatildists fled here in numbers, believing the name a sign.

## ZUILKANSIA and the worlds of the Fallen God

- **Zuilkansia**: a tribal world of basalt plateaus and warm shallow seas, close in the cosmic sea to a
  common imperial route. Oox swam to it in 2561. The **Crater of the Fall**, where the tribes found him
  (he did not fall; he climbed down the vortex wall), is a shrine.
- **Kametzor** and **Trulhex**: two of the seventeen worlds Oox conquered as a god, named in imperial
  records after syllables of his divine title. Oox was captured on Trulhex (2644).
- The other fifteen are listed in the Inquisition's *Register of the Seventeen*; among them **Gorrow**,
  **Uhlmeth** and **Sabbaroth**. The faith of the Fallen God is alive on most.

## The worlds of Cenzus Fogun

- **Ossomire**: Fogun's first conquest (2598). Its native temples were torn down and their stones used
  as the foundations of a Qothist cathedral, the model of every **Fogunian temple**. The **Burial
  Fields** of Ossomire, where tens of thousands of unconverted natives were buried alive, and where Oox
  was buried twice (2645).
- **Salsuge**: a world of salt marshes, which Fogun declared "the marsh of the prophecy" (the drained
  sea of the Eleventh Chapter). Pilgrims came to be buried with seeds.
- **Cendre** and **Vessary**: two more of his twenty-five.

The worlds on which the game is played are **former provinces** of the Cosmic Empire, or worlds it
visited without holding. They are not named here, so the game can keep them its own.

## The cosmic sea itself

- **The Southern and Northern Throats of Pyr**: the vortices. The **Throat Lights** were beacon-towers
  on the gyre's edge.
- **The Belune Lane**: the route between Pyr and Belune, 30 to 60 days.
- **The Floes**: **[proposal]** shell fragments of a hatched world, charted as navigational hazards
  after 1703 near Varam's old place.
- **The Ash-Sky**: never reached.
- **The Sunken Temple**: the ruins of the Eternal Temple on the sand-floor. One ship returned from it.

## Imperial remains on any world

What a traveller on a former province finds today, and what books will mention:

- **Aqilonic milestones** carved with "o(" and a distance in days from the world's capital.
- **Fogunian temples**: massive, grey, square-towered cathedrals built on the rubble of native
  sanctuaries, many now ruined or converted into fortresses.
- **Commanderies** of the Knights of the Cosmic Sea: star-shaped forts by the sea, near the vortex routes.
- **Rusted submarines** in harbours and on beaches, used as chapels, homes, tombs, or left alone.
- **Plague cemeteries and burial fields** of the Inquisition, which locals keep away from.
- **Imperial schools**: barracks-like buildings with a parade ground, later used as granaries or
  monasteries.
- **Salt-houses**: the Old House's (later the churches') salt and alcohol stores, where "pure" salt was
  sold. Many villages still have one.
- **Red brick**: the Balnus favoured it, and a red brick building on a former province is usually
  imperial and usually belonged to the family or its physicians.
- **Seed-graves**: Principist cemeteries whose dead were buried with a seed in hand, so that some old
  cemeteries are **orchards** or thickets.
