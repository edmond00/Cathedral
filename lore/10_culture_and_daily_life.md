# Culture and Daily Life

---

## 1. The calendar

### The week of eleven days
One day per chapter of the Principist scripture. Names are the imperial ones; peasants use shorter
forms (in brackets).

| day | chapter | name | character |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | **Silday** (Sil) | the **Silent Day**: no speech; rest; church in silence |
| 2 | 1 | **Hookday** (Hook) | first working day; courts and schools open; "a question day" |
| 3 | 2 | **Ashday** (Ash) | day for repairs, for the old and for the dead |
| 4 | 3 | **Throwday** (Throw) | market day; gambling tolerated; soldiers paid |
| 5 | 4 | **Dropday** (Drop) | day of feeling: weddings, mourning, visits |
| 6 | 5 | **Eyeday** (Eye) | judgments, audits, trials, examinations |
| 7 | 6 | **Handday** (Hand) | sowing, planting, founding, beginning things |
| 8 | 7 | **Emberday** (Ember) | smiths, bakers, fire-work; bonfires on feast weeks |
| 9 | 8 | **Feastday** (Feast) | the week's great meal; indulgence; brothels busy |
| 10 | 9 | **Sandday** (Sand) | building, masonry, the temple day of clergy |
| 11 | 10 | **Seaday** (Sea) | voyages begin; fishing; departures and returns |

Among the devout, **Silday** was kept utterly silent from −39 on. The night before, "Sil-eve", is noisy,
and the phrase "to talk like it's Sil-eve" means to chatter.

### The round
360 days on Pyr, one pulse of the embryo: the **Weeping** (the wet season, about 150 days), the
**Drought** (about 150), and the **Stillness**, about 60 cold days when the embryo is quietest, the
nearest thing the world has to winter. People speak of "the rains", "the dry" and "the cold" rather
than of seasons; "winter" is common speech for the cold on most worlds.

The imperial round is divided into **thirty-two weeks and eight days**. The eight leftover days, the
**Remnant Days**, are held at the turn of the round, outside the week, as festival. On worlds whose natural
round is not 360 days the imperial calendar drifts against the rains, and farmers keep a second, local count.

### Feasts
- **The Remnant Days** (turn of the round): eight days outside the week, when some laws were suspended;
  masques, drinking, debt-settling.
- **Delivery Day** (Aloid and Balnus era): officially the feast of "the elf prince's coming to the princess".
- **Foundation Day**: the capitulation of Dokur; parades.
- **The Sowing Feast**: first rain of the Weeping; seeds blessed for the dead.
- **Chapter-feasts**: one principium honoured per round, in rotation, so a full cycle took seven rounds.
- **The Night of the Well** (Beatildist, secret): the night the Guest was freed.
- **Salt-Silence** (Vu, after 601): a day of silence for the Silent King.

## 2. Customs by era

### The republics (to 0 FC)
Merchant oligarchies, clan democracies, a Vunic kingdom. Barons kept estates but no courts. Principism was
the fashionable faith of Benepelop's merchants from −880, and a mystery to the north.

### The Vahyrian Empire (0 to 601)
- **Religion**: many faiths coexist; the **cult of Aqilon** is the public religion. Aqilon's image (the
  one-legged emperor enthroned, crutch as sceptre) stands in every forum.
- **Culture**: the **Aqilonic cycles**: painted halls, carved friezes, recited epics of the wars (the Burning
  of Yr, the Rain Crossing, the Burial of Sorvas), all glorifying the founder.
- **Politics**: unstable; dissident factions purged, northern revolts repressed (the Hask Rising).
- **Customs**: centralised government, the Aqilonic Roads, ports, aqueducts and milestones; prosperity after
  war; the imperial post; the first imperial coinage.

### The Pyrean Empire (601 to 1321)
- **Religion**: strong control; Qothism official; others repressed.
- **Culture**: strong **agriculture** (new terraces, drainage of the Napelese marshes, imperial granaries)
  and **craftsmanship** (Penaran bridges, Avolor glass, Pebelene iron, Vunic salt-carving).
- **Politics**: strong imperial authority; governors on every province.
- **Customs**: clergy in daily life: the Silent Day enforced by fines, the **salt-house** in every village,
  seed-burial required, parish registers of silence and purity, public penances. A pious and orderly
  countryside that was also envious and informing.

### The Two-Headed Empire (1321 to 1703)
- **Religion**: Qothism and Medusosianism side by side.
- **Culture**: refined **courtly arts**, especially at the Sanatorium: **poetry** (the eleven-line *hendeca*,
  one line per chapter), **embroidery** (the Belune threadwork, from Medusosian knot-rites), **flower and
  garden arrangement** (the "steam gardens" of the Sanatorium), **ink painting** (monochrome landscapes of
  springs and mists, founded by Jelebanne).
- **Politics**: two courts, rival branches, and a politics of precedence.
- **Customs**: schools and **academies**: the imperial academy at Avolor (c. 1350), the **Academy of the
  Springs** (1362); a literate gentry; examinations for office on Eyeday.

### The Empire of the Nine Pearls (1703 to 2530)
- **Religion**: freedom of belief; Medusosian ideas at court; only Beatildism banned.
- **Culture**: a **vibrant artistic scene** with a school on each world: Blue Avorian **tide-opera** on floating
  stages; the **salt-reliefs** of Prunil; the **Aqilonian masques**; the **ice-song** of New Varam; the
  **canopy-weaving** of Green Avoria; the **dune-glass** of Golden Avoria; Avolor's great painters of giants
  (portraiture of the Balnus at full scale, on walls).
- **Politics**: relative independence of each world; the emperor a symbol of unity with weak power.
- **Customs**: **libertine** manners, especially at court and in the cities: courtesan culture, open
  marriages, masked balls on the Remnant Days, the Silent Day made optional. Popular with the cities,
  scandalous to the Principative countryside.

### The Cosmic Empire (2530 to 3579)
- **Religion**: six permitted Principist faiths; everything else punished by the Inquisition; esoteric lore
  inside the imperial family.
- **Culture**: **science and industry**: iron submarines, firearms (**[proposal]** match-lock guns and
  bombards, from c. 2600), the Guildyards, star-tables, iron roads of wagon-rails on Calvassa.
- **Politics**: strong imperial power through the Knights and the Inquisition.
- **Customs**: **uniform military education** in imperial schools on every world; **social control**
  (registers, passes, informers); cultures flattened to one; eugenics and genocide as policy. Grim, efficient,
  and literate.

### After the Hatching (0 AH onward)
A **medieval** world again: realms and free cities, manors and villages, local churches, bad roads, rusting
submarines, and old books nobody fully understands. Iron and glass are still made, and firearms survive in
a few places. Distant worlds are stars again. See `08_factions.md`.

## 3. Law and justice

- **Republics**: merchants' law in Benepelop, clan-law in Pebelos, Vunic custom.
- **Imperial law**: the **Code of Verson** (c. 40 FC), which merged the republics' laws; the **Belune Code**
  (Jelebanne, c. 1280); the **Plebeian Law** of Edilon III (2533). Many post-imperial courts still cite
  "plebeian law".
- **Religious law**: the Eleventh Chapter's rules (silence, salt, blood, age, seed) were **civil law** under
  the Pyrean and Cosmic empires.
- **Punishments**: fines; flogging; mutilation (thieves' hands, liars' tongues); exile to Varam (to 1703),
  later to Perpetua; burial alive (**the sowing**, for heresy, from c. 590; systematised by the Inquisition).
- **The Red Tribunals** judged the Ban themselves, in secret.

## 4. Money

| coin | metal | value | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **aquila** | gold | 1 aquila = 20 scutes | "the Lame": a one-legged emperor on the obverse, "o(" on the reverse. Minted from c. 10 FC. |
| **scute** | silver | 1 scute = 12 pellets | the working coin; "a scute a day" was a soldier's pay |
| **pellet** | copper | | small change |
| **salt-bar** | salt | varies | on Vu and later Prunil, a compressed salt bar was currency |
| **pearl** | gold | 1 pearl = 1½ aquila | the Nine Pearls coinage (1829), minted at Aurimont on Prunil; nine pearls round the rim |
| **ember** | gold | = 1 aquila | the Cosmic Empire's coin (2533); a crowned ember |

Post-imperial realms mint their own coins, but aquilae, pearls and embers still circulate, are hoarded, and
set the standard. A "giant's face" coin is a Balnus-era pearl or ember, showing a colossal crowned head.

## 5. Language and naming

### Languages
- **Old Vahyric**: the speech of the four republics; dialects: Benepelopite (the prestige form), Napelese,
  Penaran, Pebelene.
- **Imperial** (or **Avolorian**): the standard language of the empire, descended from Benepelopite; spoken on
  every held world by the end. Post-imperial dialects descend from it.
- **Vunic**: the tongue of the Yellow Island, unrelated to Vahyric; soft, vowel-rich, with the syllables *-vun*,
  *-vosh*, *-avi*, *-una*. Survived as a peasant tongue.
- **The Chapter-tongue**: the Ban's rendering of the elf's language, used as a liturgical language by the
  churches, much as a dead tongue is used for scripture. Nobody speaks it. The elf's actual language
  it only approximates.
- **The Academic tongue**: the learned language of the academies (a Latin-like register of Imperial), used
  in medicine, law and philosophy: *modus mentis*, *laetitia*, *testa*.
- **Belunese**, **Zuilkan**, and the languages of 297 other worlds.

### Naming
- **Vahyrians**: a given name and a family or place name: *Dusher Polosik*, *Forje Nasrop*.
- **Nobility**: given name + house, sometimes "of" a place: *Ser Gauderic Moll*, *Ermesinde of Kalom*.
- **The Ban**: given name + register numeral + house: *Naabus VII Ban-Balnus*. Old House members were simply
  *Ban*; the other houses *Ban-Balnus*, *Ban-Aloid*, *Ban-Goerim*. Favourite Ban name-sounds: *Aqilon, Verson,
  Poeloc, Teosodoc, Edilon* (Aloid); *Naabus, Diferon, Akilon, Violann, Vesifia* (Balnus).
- **Vunic**: long names built of sea-words: *Jevunavosh*, *Hanuvesh*, *Imaravun*. Surnames by origin:
  *Oskavun*, "of Osk".
- **Foundlings** of Perorigak: the surname **Perorin**.
- **Pebelenes**: given name + clan: *Vhomanor Hask*.
- **Post-imperial**: local forms, imperial names worn as saints' names or for grandeur.

## 6. Food and drink

- **Salt** is precious and controlled: pure salt from the salt-house, contraband salt from smugglers. Salting
  is done with a moment of silence on Vu, and with a blessing elsewhere.
- **Preserving** with salt or spirits bought from the church; smoking and drying (not forbidden) were the
  poor's recourse.
- **No blood**: blood-sausage, blood-pudding and rare meat were forbidden by the Eleventh Chapter and are
  still unpopular or suspect on many worlds. Butchers bleed carcasses outside the town walls.
- Staples: bread, porridge, fish, olives and oil (Benepelop), beer (Napel), mutton and cheese (Pebelos),
  salt-fish (Vu).
- Drinks: wine, beer, **spirits** (pure alcohol, from the salt-house), and the **cordial of Ban**, a tonic
  for the rich, which was elf blood cut a thousandfold, though nobody who drank it knew.

## 7. Death and burial

- **Principist**: buried with a **seed** in the hand, face up, in shallow graves so the seed can grow.
- **Medusosian**: buried **deep**, **face down**, "facing home"; in caves where possible.
- **The sowing**: heretics buried alive, standing, so "Qoth may grow truth from them".
- **Vunic**: once given to the sea; after the conquest, seed-burial with a pinch of salt.
- **Post-imperial**: all of these, in mixtures. Cemetery orchards are common.

## 8. Warfare

- **Republican era**: citizen militias, mercenaries, siege engines, clan warbands in the north.
- **Aqilonic warfare**: sapping and tunnels, fire, disease, deception, night crossings. Aqilon's principle was
  "let them in, then close the door".
- **Imperial legions**, then the **Knights of the Cosmic Sea**: disciplined infantry, heavy cavalry, marines;
  submarines for transport; from c. 2600, **[proposal]** firearms.
- **Post-imperial**: knights and men-at-arms, militias, castles built from imperial ruins.

## 9. Education

- **Old House schools** (from −880): catechism and letters.
- **Academies** (Two-Headed era): Avolor, the Springs, the Hook (Adswarist, Perorigak).
- **Imperial schools** (Cosmic Empire): compulsory, uniform, military: drill, catechism of the six faiths,
  arithmetic, Imperial, obedience.
- **Post-imperial**: monasteries, a few surviving academies, private tutors, and self-taught readers of old books.
