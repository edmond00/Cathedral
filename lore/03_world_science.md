# World Science

How the world works physically, as far as the empire's natural philosophers understood it at their
height (c. 2500 to 3500 FC), with corrections marked **[hidden]** where they were wrong. After the
Hatching most of this knowledge was lost; a post-imperial scholar knows fragments of it, usually
garbled.

---

## 1. The eggmoon

Every inhabited world is an egg laid by a giant jellyfish of the cosmic sea. Imperial anatomists (the
"testologists", from *testa*, shell) described seven layers, and the names they gave are the ones used here.

```
            cosmic sea
   ┌──────────────────────────────┐
   │  1  NIGHT SHELL              │  day and night
   │  2  RAIN SHELL               │  rain, fresh water, salt kept back
   │  3  OUTER SHELL              │  ground, seas, air: where humans live
   │  4  SUPERIOR INNER SHELL     │  bedrock; mines stop here
   │  5  HOLLOW LAYER             │  caverns, dwarfs
   │  6  INFERIOR INNER SHELL     │  the warm floor of the hollow
   │  7  EMBRYO                   │  the unborn jellyfish
   └──────────────────────────────┘
```

### 1. The night shell

The outermost shell, and the one that makes the sky. It is **not uniform**: one hemisphere, the
**Pane**, is translucent; the other, the **Veil**, is dark and pierced with minute pores. The night
shell turns slowly around the egg, independently of the rest. When the Pane is overhead it is **day**;
when the Veil is, it is **night**.

- **There is no sun.** Daylight is the diffuse glow of the embers in the ash-sky, gathered through the
  Pane. It comes from everywhere at once; shadows are soft. The brightest point of the Pane, where it
  is thinnest, is called the **Eye of Day** by common people and moves across the sky through the day.
  Folk speech, and many post-imperial texts, call it "the sun".
- **At night**, the embers show through the Veil's pores as **stars**. Other eggs, which glow faintly
  in the cosmic sea, show through as **moons**: large and distinct if near (Belune from Pyr), points if far.
- The day is not everywhere equal: near the edges of the Pane there are long **dusks**.
- The length of the day differs from world to world. Imperial standard fixed the **day of Pyr** as the
  unit, and most worlds' days fall within a tenth of it.

### 2. The rain shell

A spongy, fibrous shell that lets the cosmic sea in, slowly, as **rain**. It **strains out the salt**:
the cosmic sea is tears and is salt, but rain falls **fresh**. The salt stays in the fibres.

- **Rain rounds.** The rain shell's pores open and close with the slow pulse of the embryo (below).
  This makes the **round**: a wet season (*the Weeping*) and a dry one (*the Drought*), with a cold
  stretch when the embryo is least active. On Pyr the round is 360 days; on other worlds between about
  300 and 420.
- **Salt deposits.** Where the rain shell has cracked and slumped onto the outer shell, its salt comes
  with it. These are the only sources of salt on a world: the **mines of Osk** on the Yellow Island of
  Vu (the island is yellow from the salt-crusted sulfur of the slump), the **salt mountains of Prunil**,
  the salt marshes of **Salsuge**. Salt is therefore rare, local and precious on most worlds, which is
  what made the Salt War possible.

### 3. The outer shell

The ground. Continents, islands, seas, rivers, the air above them and everything that lives in them.
The seas of an eggmoon are **fresh water**, fed by rain and drained by the vortices.

### 4. The superior inner shell

Hard, dense, dark stone beneath the outer shell. The deepest mines of the empire stopped here: iron
tools barely scratch it. Where it is thin or broken, caves lead down.

### 5. The hollow layer

A world under the world: an immense network of caverns, lightless except for the glow of fungi and of
the embryo's warmth through the floor. The **dwarfs** live here (see `09_peoples_and_creatures.md`).
Few humans have ever reached it. The known cases are the prophet **Nemeth Oraun** of Belune (through
the Gullet), and a handful of miners and hermits on other worlds.

### 6. The inferior inner shell

The floor of the hollow layer: warm, faintly pulsing, and to the dwarfs, sacred. It is the shell of the
embryo's own chamber.

### 7. The embryo

The unborn jellyfish at the centre of the world.

- It **pulses**, and its pulse sets the rain round.
- It is **warm**, and its warmth rises through the shells; worlds are warmer below.
- **It grows by absorbing the souls of the dead of its world.** When a being dies on an eggmoon, its
  soul sinks through the shells and is taken into the embryo. **[hidden]** This is literally true. It is
  known to the dwarfs as sacred fact and to humans only as the Medusosian creed.
- When the embryo has taken enough, **the egg hatches**.

## 2. Hatching

- **What happens.** The embryo wakes, the inner shells split, the outer shell and the seas break
  apart, and the jellyfish unfolds into the cosmic sea. Everything living on the world dies at once, and
  those souls go with the others into the newborn. The world is gone. Its light vanishes from every sky
  that saw it.
- **What remains.** Shell fragments, some the size of islands, drift in the cosmic sea for a long time.
  **[proposal]** Imperial submarines charted "floes" of this kind and never understood them.
- **What triggers it.** **[hidden]** An embryo hatches when it is *full*, which is to say after enough
  deaths, so a crowded, violent world hatches sooner than an empty, peaceful one. It can also be
  **quickened**. See `12_secrets_and_mysteries.md` on the red creature that came back from the sea floor.
- **Recorded hatchings.** **Varam** (1703 FC), watched from Pyr: the bright moon swelled, clouded, and
  "unfolded like a lily" over a few nights, then drifted and faded. **Pyr** (3579 FC), watched from
  Belune and from several nearby worlds. There is no record of a hatching before 1703; it had never
  happened within sight of a literate world.
- **What the newborn does.** It swims up toward the ash-sky, grows, and in time lays eggs of its own.
  Beatildists hold that the Varam jellyfish can be seen, on certain nights, as a faint drifting veil.
  They are right.

## 3. The vortices

Every eggmoon has two **vortices**, one at each **pole**: colossal spinning waterspouts that link the
world's sea to the cosmic sea through all the outer shells.

- **The wall.** The outer wall of the vortex spins **upward**, carrying the world's water out through
  the night and rain shells into the cosmic sea. This drains the world's seas and balances the rain.
- **The eye.** Down the calm core of the vortex, cosmic sea water **falls inward**, salt and all. It
  mixes into the polar sea and makes the **brackish polar waters**, the only place a world's sea tastes
  of salt.
- **Seasons.** Both run harder in the Weeping and slacker in the Drought.
- **Approaches.** Around each vortex, for hundreds of leagues, the sea turns in a great slow gyre. Ships
  caught in it are drawn in; the old charts marked it *mare rotans*, "the turning sea", and advised
  captains to stay out.
- **Travel.** To **leave** a world, a submarine rides the wall up. To **enter** one, it drops down the
  eye. The eye is narrow and violent at its mouth; the first voyages could leave but not come back.
  Josu Exculato reached Belune in 806 and could not re-enter the Belune vortex's wall to leave again.
  The technique of **climbing the wall** from inside the eye took another generation to master.
- **Elves.** The eye also draws in whatever floats near it in the cosmic sea. This is how the Guest
  came to Pyr.

## 4. The cosmic sea

- **Composition.** Salt water: the tears of the guests of the Orgy. Warm near the ash-sky, cold near
  the sand-floor.
- **Light.** Bright near the ash-sky from the embers, dim at mid-depth, black near the floor.
- **Currents.** Slow, vast, and not understood. Eggs drift in them over ages, which is why the moons
  shift very slowly in the skies over generations. Imperial astronomers noticed this and wrote tables of it.
- **Distances.** A voyage from Pyr to Belune took 30 to 60 days; to the Avorias, 200 to 400; to New
  Varam, over 900 days. The farthest worlds of the second expansion were several thousand days out.
- **Life.** Giant jellyfish; drifting blood elves; ash elves in the sky above; **[proposal]** "sea-lice"
  of a size to hole a hull, which crews feared and nobody catalogued.
- **Pressure and depth.** Wooden submarine galleys could not go far below the level of the eggs. Only
  iron hulls (2537 onward) could descend toward the floor, and even they were lost there more often
  than not.

### The ash-sky and the embers

Above the sea: endless ash, suspended, and in it the embers of the Random Forest, which never go out.
The embers give all the light in the cosmos. Ash elves drift there. No imperial submarine ever surfaced
into it. Cosmic sea water does not end at a clear surface but thickens gradually into ash-silt, and
hulls clog.

### The sand-floor

Below the sea: the sand of the Eternal Temple, and among it the **ruins** of the temple, whatever did
not wear away, possibly including the white elves. Naabus VII's deep expeditions (3569) were the only
attempt to reach it. Three submarines were lost; the fourth came back after ten rounds with something alive.

## 6. Medicine: the humors

The empire's medicine, inherited from the physicians of the four republics and refined by the Ban,
is **humoral**. Its principles are still taught everywhere.

- The body **secretes four humors**: **blood**, **phlegm**, **yellow bile** and **black bile**. Their
  balance is health.
- **Black bile** is the dangerous one. It accumulates and cannot be purged by ordinary means; a body
  full of it wastes and dies. "Black bile corruption" is the physician's term for chronic wasting,
  melancholic decline and many fevers. Hesperine's **grey waste** was a black bile corruption.
- The body also carries **humors of the mind**, which physicians of the Two-Headed Empire named in the
  learned Latin-like tongue of the academies: *laetitia* (gladness), *melancholia*, *choler*, *nervus*
  (fear), *pudor* (shame), *voluptas* (pleasure), *constantia* (hardness), *zen* (settledness, a Vunic
  loan-word), *nausea*. Emotions are these humors rising in the body, and the **spleen** receives them.
- Food, drink and drugs are also humors taken in: alcohol, opium, salt, sugar, fat, sulfur, venom and
  so on. The mind's faculties are called *modi mentis*, "ways of the mind", and are learned.
- **Cures.** Bleeding, purging, diet, baths. **The hot springs of Belune** cure black bile corruption:
  their mineral waters draw it out. That is the whole reason Belune was colonised.
- **Elf blood** (below) is the one cure the physicians could not explain.

## 7. Elf blood

What the blood of a blood elf does to a human body, as learned by the Oskavun twins, the Ban, and above
all the Red Brick House over 4,600 rounds.

| dose | effect | who |
|---|---|---|
| a single draught | cures most diseases; heals wounds fast; lengthens life to ~200 rounds; the body grows tall (~2 m) and takes faint elvish features | the Counsel (Ban-Aloid), once, on the **1100th day** of life |
| a sip every 11 days | life to ~500 rounds; the body **stops growing and keeps ageing in appearance**: wrinkled, frail-looking, hairless, but not weakened | the Old House, from ordination |
| daily, as sole food | life past **2,000 rounds**; the body **never stops growing**: first tall, then gigantic, then deformed (extra limbs, disproportion, fur, fused fingers) | the Red Brick House (Ban-Balnus) |
| direct, unrefined | as daily, but violent: rejuvenation within days, then fevers ("red fever"), madness in some | the twins; Ansedor; Balnus (who died of it) |
| cut a thousandfold | a strong tonic; eases fevers, closes wounds, delays black bile | the "cordial of Ban", sold for a millennium |

- **Growth.** The blood pushes the body to keep making itself. In a short life this is healing; in a
  long one it is **growth without bound**. Lwrash was four metres tall at 300; Oox ten metres at 900;
  Vesifia III twenty-five metres at 1,000.
- **Sterility.** Over many generations of incest and daily blood the Balnus became barren. The last Ban
  birth was Vesifia III in 2579 FC.
- **Refining.** The Balnus learned to **refine** the blood: settling, filtering through salt and linen,
  cutting with wine or milk, and heating to exact warmth. Refined blood keeps longer and kills less.
  Their recipes were the most closely held secret in the empire, and none survive.
- **Supply.** One elf gives a limited amount, drawn from the veins of the arms and thighs on a fixed
  schedule, which Balnus physicians called "the tide". The elf heals completely between draws.
- **[hidden]** Elf blood is not medicine in the humoral sense. It carries the Orgy's nature: *more,
  always more, never ending*. It feeds that into whoever drinks it.

## 8. Reckoning time

- **The day** is the only natural unit everyone uses. Peasants, soldiers and merchants count in days:
  "a hundred days' march", "three thousand days in the cell".
- **The week** is **11 days**, one for each chapter (0 to 10). Adopted by Principists c. −800; imposed
  empire-wide after 262 FC. The first day, the **Silent Day** (Chapter 0), became a day of silence after
  the Eleventh Chapter. Day names: see `10_culture_and_daily_life.md`.
- **The round** is one full rain cycle, 360 days on Pyr. Scholars and chanceries count rounds; ordinary
  people rarely do. Other worlds had their own round lengths; the empire imposed Pyr's, which on most
  worlds drifts out of step with the rain, and so the imperial calendar and the farmer's calendar never agree.
- **Eras.** Principist chronicles count from the Delivery (**Rounds of the Blood**, secret, used only by
  the Red Brick House), from the completion of the Ten Chapters (**Rounds of the Word**, used by the
  clergy), or from the Foundation (**Foundation Count**, imperial). Vu counted by the reigns of its
  Tide-Kings, and Belune by the Sanatorium. After the Hatching most worlds count **from the Hatching**, or
  from the founding of their own local realm, or not at all.
