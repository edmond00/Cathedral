# Texts and Sources

The player learns the lore only from **sources inside the world**. This file lists the kinds of sources
that exist, the major named texts, and for each major event **the versions in circulation**: who tells
it which way, and why.

---

## 1. Palimpsests

Vellum was expensive and the Principian Inquisition (2533 to 3579) burned or **scraped** forbidden books.
A scraped leaf was washed with milk and pumice and rewritten, usually with something pious. The ink of
the old text sinks into the skin, and under a raking light or with age it **comes back**, faint and
brownish, beneath the new.

So many surviving books are **palimpsests**: a permitted text (a Genesive homily, an imperial law, a
catechism) over a **proscribed** one (a Beatildist hymn, a Medusosian descent-rite, a native chronicle, an
Adswarist doubt about the Eleventh Chapter). After the Hatching the Inquisition was gone, and readers
began to prize the lower text above the upper.

**For the game**: any book can have two layers, disagreeing.

---

## 2. Kinds of sources

| kind | typical content | typical bias |
|---|---|---|
| **Scripture** | the Ten Chapters, the Eleventh Chapter, commentaries | church doctrine; the Eleventh is presented as authentic |
| **Imperial chronicles** | reigns, wars, foundations | the reigning house; Aloid chronicles glorify Aqilon, Balnus chronicles erase the Aloid |
| **Church histories** | schisms, councils, saints | each church's own succession |
| **Heretical tracts** | Adswarist doubts, Principative polemic, Beatildist martyrology | against the Old House, the Balnus, the Genesive church |
| **Romances and plays** | the knights of Ban, the Princess and the Seagull, the Burning of Yr | drama over accuracy; censored in some eras, tolerated in others |
| **Songs and ballads** | Vhomanor, the Silent King, the Awakened Prince | local and national memory |
| **Travellers' relations** | Kaeros Lougi, Josu's journal, Arneviz Geoant | wonder; misunderstanding; self-promotion |
| **Medical treatises** | humors, the springs, the cordial of Ban | learned; they never know what the cordial is |
| **Legal codes** | Code of Verson, Belune Code, Plebeian Law | the state's categories |
| **Inquisition records** | trials, sowings, confiscations | procedure, confession under torture |
| **Folk tales** | witches, giants, goblins, deep folk | memory worn down to motif |
| **Inscriptions** | milestones, temple dedications, tombs | propaganda; fragments |

## 3. Named texts

### Scripture and religion
- **The Ten Chapters** (*the Hesperine Codex*), Hesperine and Oudin Ban, completed −903. The core scripture;
  eleven texts numbered 0 to 10, the first blank.
- **The Eleventh Chapter**, "recovered" −40, in fact forged by Aos Ban and Aqilon Ban-Aloid in −41. The
  prophecy of the marshes and the Garden; the five rules; forgiveness for gold.
- **The Acts of the Convocation**, 262. The First Schism's decrees.
- **Firaperc's *Two Faces***, 1003. Survives only in fragments quoted by those who refuted it.
- **Alos Ban-Goerim, *On the Sole Hand***, c. 1020. Genesive doctrine.
- **Adelim Ban-Goerim, *The Mind of Qoth***, c. 1025. Figurative doctrine, in verse.
- **Dusher Polosik, *Plain Questions***, c. 1030. Principative doctrine. Calls the Eleventh "a merchant's chapter".
- **Nemeth Oraun, *The Descent***, oral, written down on Belune c. 900 FC. Medusosian scripture.
- **The *Hours of the Veiled Empress***, Beatildist hymnal, c. 1750. Circulated in palimpsest.
- **Amaury Chastel, *Manual of Sowing***, 2540. The Inquisition's procedure.

### Chronicles and histories
- **The *Deeds of the Lame Emperor***, court of Verson II, c. 50. The official account of the Eleventh
  Chapter and the Wars: Verson murdered by the Chapter's enemies; Aqilon a reluctant saviour; the
  imprisonment passed over in one line ("the emperor, worn by his wounds, withdrew from the world").
- **The *Ash Chronicle of Yr***, Napelese, written in secret c. 120. The Wars from the losers' side:
  Lwrash's plague, the burning, "the Lame Butcher".
- **The *Lay of the Shoulder***, Pebelene, oral, fixed in writing c. 300. The epic of Vhomanor.
- **The *Annals of the Salt Palace***, the continuous imperial annals, 601 to 1703, partly destroyed in
  the Red Night.
- **The *Litany of the Island***, Imaravun of Vu ("Sister Salt"), c. 640. A Qothist nun's prayer-book that
  is, read closely, a lament for Vu and her father.
- **Clothilde Geoant (ed.), *Relations of the Far Worlds***, c. 2875. Arneviz's voyages.
- **Gereston Bolish, *Northern Letters***, c. 1830.
- **The *Register of Worlds***, the IISTG's count of 299 visited and 229 held, c. 3100.
- **Captain Idrenne Sauval, *Log of the Patience of Rhesis***, 3569 to 3579. Survives in fragments of the
  last dispatches copied at Belune.

### Voyages and science
- **Kaeros Lougi, *Out Through the Throat***, 780. The first account of the cosmic sea.
- **Josu Exculato, *Journal*** (found in a cairn on Belune by Barnoix, 833).
- **The *Tables of Barnutum***, star-tables, 840 onward, revised to c. 3400: 383 moons listed.
- **Arnest Goprufid, *On Going Under***, 760. The submarine galley.
- **The *Testology*** (anon., Academy of the Hook, c. 2700). The anatomy of eggmoons; the seven shells.

### Romances, plays, songs
- **The *Four Knights of Ban***, a romance of the Two-Headed era. The witches are sirens; the golden knight
  is written out; the black knight becomes the tragic hero.
- **The *Princess and the Seagull***, Nine Pearls era, tolerated. Beatilda as lovelorn heroine, Jhoeland as
  tragic betrayer; the elf is a "sorcerer" she wished to redeem.
- **The *Tide-Opera of Ysmé***, Blue Avorian, after 2530, banned. The courtesan who killed a giant.
- **The *Ballad of the Silent King***, Vunic.
- **The *Song of the Wakeful***, the Avolor poor's song of the Awakened Prince.

## 4. The versions of each event

For every event: **what happened** (see the other files), then the versions a book might give.

### The origin of the Ban
- **Official (Aloid and Balnus alike)**: an **elf prince** came out of the sea to the house of the Baron of
  Ban and loved his daughter, the **Princess** Hesperine; their children are the half-elves. The elf's blood
  in their veins gives them long life.
- **Red Brick archives (secret)**: the whole truth: the twins, the knights, the chained Guest, the incest.
- **Vunic tale**: two sisters of Tavunesh kept a sea-man in a net-shed and grew old without ageing, until
  foreign knights came and killed them all. Vunic tellers do not connect it with the Ban.
- **Romance (*Four Knights of Ban*)**: the knights sought a "sea-physic"; sirens; the black knight's doom;
  the princess cured by "a fountain" the golden knight found.
- **Beatildist**: the Ban are the jailers and drinkers of a martyr; the "elf prince" is a lie told over a chain.

### The Eleventh Chapter
- **Orthodox (Early Qothism, Genesive)**: a lost chapter recovered by Aos Ban from the archives; the
  Chapter's enemies murdered its sponsor Verson; the people rose for their faith.
- **Adswarist (whispered)**: the style is wrong; the chapter speaks of salt-houses that did not exist in −903.
- **Principative**: "a merchant's chapter", written to sell salt and forgiveness. Dusher said so openly.
- **Figurative**: the chapter is true as metaphor whatever its origin: the garden is Qoth's mind.
- **The Wakeful**: the Prince saw the Garden; the chapter was true and the Ban stole it.
- **Truth**: forged by Aos and Aqilon; Verson killed by Aqilon because he had found it out.

### The Aqilonic Wars
- **Imperial (*Deeds of the Lame Emperor*)**: a just war against aggressors; the "cleansing fire" of Yr; the
  "honourable surrender" of Vhomanor (depicted kneeling).
- **Napelese (*Ash Chronicle*)**: Lwrash's plague-catapults; the burning of Yr with its people; the Lame Butcher.
- **Pebelene (*Lay of the Shoulder*)**: Vhomanor never knelt; the mountain was brought down on him because
  no one could beat him.
- **Popular song**: "the son who did not know his father".
- **Truth**: all of the above, except the kneeling.

### The death of Aqilon I
- **Aloid chronicle**: he "withdrew from the world, worn by his wounds" and died.
- **Folk**: his son did not recognise him and locked him up as an impostor.
- **Some Balnus-era chronicles**: the man at the gate really was an impostor, and Aqilon died at Dokur.
- **Truth**: Verson II knew him and imprisoned him anyway.

### The First Schism
- **Orthodox**: the Old House, guided by the aged and saintly Aos, fixed the faith against the burners.
- **Children of Rede**: Grizx was the true prophet, and the Convocation murdered his children.
- **Truth**: Aos, the forger of the Eleventh Chapter, fixed orthodoxy himself; Grizx never preached sacrifice.

### The Salt War
- **Imperial**: Vu insulted the faith and starved the empire; a just war; the galleys a triumph of the IISTG.
- **Vunic**: the Silent King refused to break his silence for foreigners; they buried him under his own salt.
- **Principative**: the sowing was a crime; Verester II a zealot.

### The Second Schism
- **Genesive**: the fire was Qoth's judgment on the heresy of the two faces; Alos inherited the true authority.
- **Figurative**: the fire judged the *form* of Firaperc's thesis, not its truth; Adelim inherited its spirit.
- **Principative**: the fire judged the whole house, which had failed; Qoth does not intervene, so it was an
  accident, and the house deserved no better.
- **Truth**: an accident in a library stove.

### Belune
- **Imperial**: a civilising mission and a gift of health; Jelebanne the saintly queen.
- **Belunese Remnant**: the sick came from the sky and killed us.
- **Medusosian**: the plagues sent many souls home.

### The Elf and the Princess
- **Balnus official**: Princess Beatilda died in a fire in 1702. (There is nothing more.)
- **Red Tribunal record**: Beatilda, alive, plotted a coup with a hired assassin, killed Akilon VIII, and was
  exiled; the emperor was killed by her accomplices. The Aloid were purged as conspirators.
- **Romance**: a love story; the elf is a sorcerer or an enchanted prince.
- **Beatildist**: the true empress freed the martyr and was betrayed; the Grandmother murdered her son-in-law;
  Varam hatched to receive her; she reigns in the sea.
- **Truth**: see `05_figures.md`. The Beatildists are nearly right, except that the hatching of Varam was
  not for her.

### The Assassination of Akilon XII
- **Edilonist**: the Ticklers' Guild killed him; Bemakor and the Red Tribunals tried to seize the throne.
- **Belunese**: Bemakor, the lawful regent, was betrayed by Karmon.
- **Blue Avorian song**: Ysmé, alone, for her own reasons.
- **Truth**: Bemakor paid a Tickler cell, which used Ysmé.

### Oox
- **Inquisition**: a heretic, traitor and false god, twice buried.
- **Faith of the Fallen God**: the god who fell, conquered, was buried, rose, was buried, and will rise.
- **Imperial family**: a monstrous cousin; the proof of what the blood does.

### The Hatching of Pyr
- **Principist (Genesive)**: Repance's judgment; the beginning of the draining of the sea.
- **Figurative**: Qoth has turned its thought away from Pyr.
- **Principative**: nothing but what the world is: things end.
- **Medusosian**: the god of Pyr was born; every soul of Pyr is in it now.
- **Beatildist**: her vengeance.
- **Esoteric**: the elf of the temple did it. (Right, but not in the way they think.)
- **Folk**: Pyr "went out like a candle".

### Goblins
- **Folk**: children of the dead empire, cave-demons, stolen children turned wild.
- **Clergy**: Repance's vermin.
- **Scholars who read the last dispatches**: descendants of the red creature from the sea floor.

## 5. How to write a book for the game

1. Pick an **author** (a person, an institution, a tradition) and an **era**.
2. Take **what happened** from these files.
3. Apply the author's **bias** from the tables above: what they cannot know, what they must not say, what
   they want believed.
4. Consider a **palimpsest**: a second text beneath, from an enemy of the first.
5. Leave **gaps**. No in-world source knows everything, and most know very little.
