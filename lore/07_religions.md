## 1. Principism

**Founded** c. −880 by the Ban, from the Ten Chapters (written −960 to −903). It is a **mixture**:
the elf's account of the beginning, as the Ban understood it, poured into the older piety of
Benepelop: its household rites for the dead, its magistrates' reverence for judgment (which became
Rhesis), its sailors' luck-cults (which became Rede), and the Ban's own taste for questions. **Scripture**: the Ten
Chapters (0 to 10) and, from −39, the Eleventh Chapter. **Clergy**: the Old House until 1004; then the
three churches. **Core belief**: the cosmos is a chain of necessary answers; the world we live in is
the fourth and last creation, made of the remains of the three that were destroyed; Qoth made it and
Repance will unmake it.

### Common practice (all branches)

- **The 11-day week**: one day per chapter, the first (Chapter 0, Silance) set apart.
- **The Silent Day**: from −39 (Eleventh Chapter), no speech on the first day of the week. Enforced by
  law under the Pyrean empire; loosened under the Balnus (1704), enforced again by the Inquisition (2533).
  Merchants, soldiers and midwives had dispensations, which the church sold. Sign-languages developed:
  "silent-hand" is still used by some post-imperial peasants who have forgotten why.
- **Seed burial**: the dead are buried with a **seed** in their hand (Eleventh Chapter), to plant in
  Qoth's garden. Which seed matters: grain for the humble, fruit-stones for the wealthy, rare seeds for
  the great. Old cemeteries became orchards and thickets.
- **Pure salt and pure alcohol**: food may only be preserved with salt and spirits bought from the
  church's **salt-houses**. This monopoly was the Old House's wealth, and later the churches'.
- **The blood taboo**: no contact with blood, animal or human. Butchers, surgeons, midwives and
  soldiers were ritually impure and paid a **purification** (a tithe, a fast, a washing with pure salt
  water). Blood-sausages, blood-brotherhood and bloodletting by laymen were forbidden. **[hidden]** The
  taboo protected the Ban's monopoly on blood-knowledge, and was written by a family that drank blood.
- **The age rule**: no union in which the woman is older than the man. Widely broken, widely confessed,
  and a steady source of forgiveness-fees and village scandal.
- **Forgiveness for gold**: sins against the Eleventh Chapter could be "forgotten" for a tribute paid to
  the Old House, later to the churches. The Principative church abolished it.
- **Iconography**: the seven symbols (blank page, hook, hourglass, knucklebones, drop, eye, open hand) and
  the four worlds (ember, tear, grain of sand, wave). **No images of the principia as persons** in
  orthodox art; popular art ignored this.
- **Prayer** takes the form of **questions**. A Principist does not ask for things; they ask *why*, in
  the form of Adswaru's questions, and meditate on the answer. "Why must this exist, if it will
  disappear?" is said over the sick.

### The afterlife (after −39)

The Eleventh Chapter's prophecy: Repance will one day drain the cosmic sea into **marshes**, and Qoth will
plant a **garden** on them. The souls of the virtuous dead, judged by Qoth, will live there in eternal
happiness, and each will plant the seed they were buried with. The rest are "forgotten", which means
simply that they will not exist.

**[hidden]** There is no garden. The souls of the dead sink into the embryo of their world.

### Before 262: the sects

Before the First Schism, Principism was a swarm of independent congregations, and some worshipped a
single principium as a god:
- **The Rede-cults**, gamblers', sailors' and soldiers' fraternities of the Throw. The **Children of Rede**
  (249 to 263) burnt people alive "to return them to chance".
- **The Rhema-mysteries**: ecstatic women's congregations that wept together; accused of orgies (the
  Cosmic Orgy is Rhema's world).
- **The Rhesian cult of magistrates**: judges and physicians who venerated the Watcher; absorbed into
  Early Qothism.
- **The Repance ascetics**: hermits who let their bodies and houses decay unrepaired; tolerated as eccentrics.
- **The Hesperinians**: devotees of the Princess as Scribe; absorbed into Qothism, which kept her
  as a saint.

## 2. The branches after the First Schism (262)

Fixed by the **Convocation of Avolor** under Poeloc III and Aos Ban.

### Early Qothism (official, 262 to 1004)
**Qoth is the main principium**, because Qoth made the world we live in and the garden we hope for. The
other principia are real and venerable but secondary. It was the religion of the Old House, the empire
and the salt-houses. Its liturgy was built round the Eleventh Chapter.

### Adswarism (tolerated)
**Reason above all; Adswaru is the main principium**, since without the question nothing would have been
answered. Adswarists are philosophers, lawyers, engineers and physicians. Suspicious of revelation, they
quietly doubted the Eleventh Chapter, and the Adswarist **Academy of the Hook** at Perorigak produced the
empire's best natural philosophy. Under the Cosmic Empire the IISTG's engineers were Adswarists almost to
a man. It was tolerated throughout and never trusted.

### Silihism (tolerated)
**Silance is the main principium, and reality does not exist**: everything after Chapter 0 is a
disturbance of the blank page, and the wise return to it. Silihists are contemplatives, mutes, hermits,
suicides. Their monasteries, the **Blank Houses**, keep perpetual silence, which made the Silent Day
easy for them. Tolerated because harmless. Vunic Stillness blended with Silihism after the conquest of Vu.

### Principlurism (tolerated)
**No principium is main**; all seven are one chain and none can be removed. A faith of synthesis and
compromise, popular with merchants and diplomats. It later absorbed many of the other worlds' polytheisms
by treating native gods as "local principia".

## 3. The branches after the Second Schism (1004)

The Old House burned during the council on Firaperc's thesis of the **two faces of Qoth**: that Qoth is
both one principium among seven *and* the omnipotent maker. Everyone read the fire as Qoth's verdict. They
disagreed about what it had judged.

### Genesive Qothism, founded by Alos Ban-Goerim (see at Avolor)
**Qoth is the sole creator and sustainer of the cosmic sea. The other principia ceased to exist once
the sea was made, and Qoth alone remains, omnipotent.** The others were stages, as scaffolding is to a
house. The most hierarchical and legalistic church: primates, bishops, salt-houses, forgiveness-fees. The
**imperial church** for most of the empire's later history, and the church of the **Inquisition**.
Cenzus Fogun was Genesive.

### Figurative Qothism, founded by Adelim Ban-Goerim (see at Perorigak)
**Qoth is the sole omnipotent creator and sustainer of the cosmic sea; the other principia are Qoth's
inner thoughts**: Silance is Qoth's silence, Adswaru Qoth's questioning, Repance Qoth's grief at
impermanence, and so on. The Ten Chapters are the story of a mind thinking. A church of poets, mystics
and contemplative orders, strong in Penaros and at the Belune court. The Two-Headed Empire's refined
culture was largely Figurative.

### Principative Qothism, founded by Dusher Polosik (the Plain Church of Lusk)
**All the principia exist for eternity. Qoth is not omnipotent, and Qoth cannot intervene in the present
world; but Qoth remains the main principium**, the one who made us and will not come back to mend what
breaks. No miracles, no forgiveness for gold, no salt monopoly (a Principative salts with whatever salt
there is and says so), no priest between a person and the questions. Dusher called the Eleventh Chapter
"a merchant's chapter" but kept the seed burial, since "a seed does no harm". The church of the poor, the
river-folk, and later of many colonies. Plain chapels, no altar, no images, no bells.

### Residual branches
Adswarism, Silihism and Principlurism survived beside the three Qothisms. Under the Cosmic Empire these
**six** were the only legal faiths (see below).

## 5. Medusosianism, the faith of the jellyfish

**Origin**: the religion of the **dwarfs** of the hollow layers, brought up among humans on Belune by the
prophet **Nemeth Oraun** (c. −900 FC), who went down the Gullet and lived among the dwarfs of Oggerund.

**Belief**: 
- Every world is an egg of the cosmic jellyfish, and the **embryo** at its heart is a god being born.
- When a creature dies, its **soul sinks** through the shells into the embryo, which grows on the souls
  of the dead.
- When it has gathered enough souls it **hatches**, and all the souls of the world are **unified** in a
  god-like jellyfish, swimming free in the endless sea. This is salvation: not a garden, a becoming-one.
- Death is therefore not an end but a **descent**. The dead are buried **deep**, face down, "facing home".
- Life should be lived so that one's soul is **worth taking**, which means full, intense, remembered.
  (Nine Pearls libertines found this convenient.)

**Practice**: prayer lying face-down on the earth ("listening to the heart"); pilgrimages to caves and
deep places; funeral descents into caves; the **veil** (a translucent fabric representing the bell of the
jellyfish) worn by priestesses; the **threadcount**, a rite of knotted cords recalling the tentacles.

**History**: native faith of Belune → reached Pyr with the Belune Traffic (c. 850) → accepted at the
Two-Headed courts → fashionable in the Nine Pearls imperial family → **proscribed** by the Inquisition
(2533) → survived underground and in the imperial family's esoteric circles → openly revived on many
worlds after the Hatching, which it seemed to explain.

**[hidden]** Medusosianism is the only human religion whose core claim is literally true.

### Beatildism (from 1703)
A Medusosian heresy. **Beatilda**, the rightful empress, murdered by her own family for trying to free the
elf, was **taken up by the embryo of Varam** days after her arrival, and Varam hatched *because of her*,
to receive her. She lives on as the god-jellyfish of Varam, the **Veiled Empress**, and will return.

- Believers hold the **Ban** to be devourers, the **elf** a martyr, and the Balnus throne illegitimate.
- They watch for the Varam jellyfish in the night sky (it can in fact be seen, faintly).
- They keep the **Night of the Well** (the night the elf was freed) as their chief feast.
- **Severely proscribed** from 1703 by every regime until the Hatching. Members met in "wells" (cells),
  used the **seagull**'s broken feather as a sign of betrayal and a red thread as a sign of recognition.
- Many fled to **New Varam**. After the Hatching, Beatildists regarded the end of Pyr as her vengeance.

## 6. The Stillness of Vu

The ancient spiritual discipline of the Yellow Island. It has **no gods** and no scripture.

- Practice: **sitting**, still and silent, facing the sea, breathing with the waves, until the mind is
  "as still as the salt in the rock". The court of Vu sat each morning in the Sitting Hall.
- Teaching, passed mouth to mouth by **tide-masters**: the world moves, the self need not; stillness is
  strength; grief and greed are waves.
- The Principist silent day seemed to the Vunic a crude imitation, and they refused to keep it "on
  command", which is why their merchants were buried in 593.
- After the conquest, the Stillness survived as a private practice, partly absorbed into Silihism.
  Vunic salt-miners keep a moment of silence when salting food, in memory of the Silent King.
- "**Zen**", the humor of settledness in imperial medicine, is a Vunic word.

## 7. The Faith of the Fallen God (from 2566)

The cult of **Oox Ban-Balnus**, worshipped on Zuilkansia and sixteen other worlds as **Bigruw Trulhexiwn
Kametzoriis**, "the god fallen from the sky", in the Zuilkan tongue.

- The god fell from the sea above, was not drowned, conquered, was buried by the "grey priests" (the
  Inquisition), **rose from the earth**, was buried again, and **will rise again**.
- Temples are **pits**; worshippers climb down into them. Relics are fragments of giant bone (not his).
- Practice includes feats of strength, burial-and-rising initiations (the initiate is buried alive for a
  night), and war.
- Repressed by the Inquisition; alive on most of the seventeen worlds; flourishing after the Hatching.

## 8. Esoteric and occult lore ("the Far Lore")

Brought to court by **Arneviz Geoant** (2815 to 2871) from the farthest worlds, and cultivated inside the
imperial family and among a few noble circles, the **Far Lodges**, under the Cosmic Empire while the
population was held to the six churches.

- **Elf-science**: the belief that the elves possessed a medicine, a language and a mathematics from before
  the cosmic sea, recoverable from relics. It is what sent Naabus VII's expeditions to the sea floor.
- **The speaking stones of Ysthane**: monoliths believed to record the voices of the dead.
- **Hollow-cults** from several worlds, related to Medusosianism.
- **Numerology of the eleven**: the eleven chapters, the 11-day week, the 1100th day.
- Geoant's disappearance made him a prophet to the Lodges; they expect him to return from beyond the tables.

## 9. Native faiths of the conquered worlds

The 299 visited worlds had their own religions, mostly polytheist, many animist, several centred on caves
or the sky. The Nine Pearls tolerated them. The Cosmic Empire forbade them, destroyed their temples and
built **Fogunian temples** on the rubble. They survived in folk custom, in saints' cults grafted onto
Principism, and, after the Hatching, in open revival. On a former province today, a village's "Principist"
feast is often much older than Principism.

---

## 10. After the Hatching

- The churches lost their centre (their sees were on Pyr) and split into local churches under local
  primates, each claiming the true succession.
- Many held the Hatching to be **Repance's** work. Some held it to be the beginning of the draining of the
  sea, and so the coming of the Garden, and waited for the marshes.
- Medusosianism revived and spread: the end of Pyr looked exactly like what it had always said.
- New syncretic sects appeared on nearly every world. Some worship the **Last Empress**. Some fear or
  worship **goblins**.
