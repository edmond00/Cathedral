# The Lore in Summary

## I. The Cosmos

### The Principia

Before anything, there were the **Principia**: god-like entities, each one the answer to a question.
A principium asks another principium a question, and the answer is a new principium.

| # | Principium | domain | born of |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Silance** | nothing | (none; the first chapter is blank) |
| 2 | **Adswaru** | reason, the axioms of existence | (none; it is the one who asks) |
| 3 | **Repance** | time and destruction | Adswaru asks Silance: *why does something exist rather than nothing?* "What exists will disappear into nothing over time." |
| 4 | **Rede** | randomness and chaos | Adswaru asks Repance: *if what exists always disappears, why must it exist at all?* "Disappearance is random, undetermined by the thing's nature; so for destruction to happen, the thing must first exist." |
| 5 | **Rhema** | emotions and sensations | Adswaru asks Repance: *why does disappearance itself exist, and not only the thing that disappears?* "When things disappear, something will be felt: pain or relief, regret or contentment." |
| 6 | **Rhesis** | observation and judgment | Adswaru asks Repance: *who will feel what when something disappears?* "I will watch destruction and judge which feeling befits which thing." |
| 7 | **Qoth** | reality | Adswaru asks Repance: *what will disappear?* "I will create what will be destroyed." |

In every case **the one asked gives the answer**, and the answer becomes the new principium, which
then speaks it in its own voice. Silance answers Chapter 2 and Repance answers Chapters 3 to 6.
Adswaru only asks, until Chapter 10, when it answers Qoth.

### The four worlds

Qoth did not answer with principia but with **worlds**, each one made with the counsel of another
principium, and each one found by Repance.

1. **The Random Forest** (with Rede): an endless forest growing chaotically in every direction.
   Repance **burnt** it to **ashes and embers**.
2. **The Cosmic Orgy** (with Rhema): a limitless feast of endless dishes and guests of infinite beauty.
   Repance let it **rot**: the food spoiled, the guests grew old and ugly and wept, and their weeping
   became **tears**.
3. **The Eternal Temple** (with Rhesis): a temple of marble and gold where Qoth sat enthroned to judge
   all things. Repance **wore** its walls down to **sand**.
4. **The Cosmic Sea** (with Adswaru): Qoth showed the ashes, the tears and the sand to Adswaru, who
   said: *the ashes up, the sand down, the tears between.* The tears became an endless sea, lit from
   above by the embers of the forest floating in the ash-sky, and floored below by the sand of the temple.

This account is the **Ten Chapters**, numbered 0 to 10: eleven texts, the first one blank.

### The elves

Each of the first three worlds had its people, and they were **immortal**: they outlived their worlds.
- **Wood elves**, living plants with skin like thin bark, became **ash elves**, skin like coal, when
  the forest burnt. They drift in the ash-sky among the embers.
- **Blood elves**, the guests of the orgy, skin like human skin. They float unconscious in the cosmic
  sea, adrift in the tears they wept. Their blood restores and prolongs life.
- **White elves**, servitors and worshippers of Rhesis, skin like marble. They are presumed to lie in
  the sand of the sea floor among the ruins of the temple.

### The cosmic sea and the eggmoons

In the cosmic sea live **giant jellyfish**, and they lay **giant eggs**. Every inhabited world is one
of these eggs, an **eggmoon**. From outside in:

> **night shell** (makes day and night) › **rain shell** (lets the sea in as fresh rain; keeps its
> salt) › **outer shell** (ground and seas, where humans live) › **superior inner shell** ›
> **hollow layer** (where the dwarfs live) › **inferior inner shell** › **the embryo**

At each pole a **vortex**, a colossal waterspout, links the egg's sea to the cosmic sea. Through
the vortices, **submarines** can leave a world and reach another.

**[hidden]** The embryo grows by drawing in the souls of the dead of its world. When it has taken
enough, the egg **hatches**, the world ends, and a new god-like jellyfish swims away carrying every
soul it gathered. The dwarfs have always known this; they worship it. Humans learned it as a
religion (**Medusosianism**) and never as a fact.

## II. The Ban Empire

### Pyr

**Pyr** is an eggmoon with one crescent continent, **Vahyr**, and one island, the **Yellow Island**
of the **Kingdom of Vu**. Seen on a map it is "o(": Vu to the west across the **Sea of Vu**, Vahyr
curving around the **Inland Seas** to the east. Vahyr was split into four republics, south to north:
**Benepelop** (Avolor, Kalom), **Napel** (Yr), **Penaros** (Perorigak, Perostro) and **Pebelos**
(Avenas, Sorvas, Dokur). The nearest moon is **Belune**, which looks large in the sky; the brightest
is icy **Varam**.

### The fishing hut (−1203 to −1052 FC)

A blood elf, drawn out of the cosmic sea through the eye of Pyr's south vortex, drifted to the
western shore of Vu and was caught in the net of two twin fisherwomen, **Hanuvesh and Hanuvela
Oskavun**. They chained it in their hut, drank its blood and stopped ageing, and for a hundred and
fifty rounds sold its blood to the villages as a cure. They were the "witches of the fishing hut".

**Ansedor, Baron of Ban**, a landed noble of Benepelop, had a daughter, **Hesperine**, dying of the
grey waste. He sent four knights for a remedy. The **white knight** was trapped and killed by the
witches. The **black knight** was charmed and became their protector. The **blue knight** was killed
by the black. Then the black knight, sick with remorse, killed the twins, burnt their bodies and
killed himself. The **golden knight** found the corpses and the chained elf and brought it home.

The elf's blood healed Hesperine. Ansedor drank it and grew young. He took his daughter as his wife,
and from that union came the **Ban**, a dynasty that would call itself **the half-elves** and claim
to descend from an elf prince and a human princess. It kept the elf chained for the rest of its
existence.

### The houses of Ban (−1052 to −40 FC)

The Ban became physicians, scholars and advisors. Long life let them accumulate knowledge and
influence. They learned the elf's language, questioned it about the beginning of things, and around
−903 wrote down the **Ten Chapters**. From this grew **Principism**, and the Ban became its
priesthood, strongest in Benepelop. Vu kept its own meditative faith, **the Stillness**.

In −512 the patriarch **Oudin** died, leaving three sons, and the family split into three houses:

| house | name | head | office | blood |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Old House** | Ban | patriarch | the clergy: teaching and interpreting the Chapters | a sip every 11th day after ordination; ~500 rounds of life, looking ever older |
| **The Red Brick House** | Ban-Balnus | matriarch | the keepers: guard the elf, draw and refine its blood, sell its medicine | blood daily and nothing else; 2000+ rounds of life; they grow into deformed giants |
| **The Counsel** | Ban-Aloid | patriarch | advisors to the noble houses in politics, war and history | one draught on their 1100th day; ~200 rounds of life, 2 m tall, faintly elvish |

The Counsel was eventually no longer told that the draught came from a living prisoner.

### The Eleventh Chapter (−40 FC)

The Old House had dwindled to its young patriarch **Aos Ban** and a few ancients. With his cousin
and friend **Aqilon Ban-Aloid**, a member of the **Ekklesia of Benepelop** (thirty-three members),
Aos forged an **Eleventh Chapter**: Repance will drain the cosmic sea into marshes, Qoth will plant a
garden on them, and the virtuous dead will live there forever. To enter the garden one must eat only
food preserved with the Old House's pure salt and alcohol, never lie with an older woman, never
touch blood, be buried with a seed in one's hand, and keep silence every eleventh day. Sins could be
forgiven for gold.

Aqilon's elder brother **Verson** died on the morning of the vote. The chapter failed by one vote.
**[hidden]** Verson had found out the forgery, and Aqilon poisoned him before he could speak.
Benepelop's devout rose, led by a mystic calling himself **the Awakened Prince**. Five of the
Ekklesia's no-voters were lynched. The frightened survivors gave Aqilon emergency powers. He
massacred the rioters, imprisoned the Prince, purged the Ekklesia, and had the chapter adopted. He
ruled Benepelop as a tyrant for nearly thirty rounds.

### The Aqilonic Wars (−12 to 0 FC)

**Napel** declared war on the heretic tyrant and took **Kalom**. The expelled Balnus giant **Lwrash**
retook it by catapulting corpses over the walls until plague did the work. While Napel reinforced
Kalom, Aqilon took **Yr**; Napel capitulated. **Penaros and Pebelos** besieged Yr for about 340 days.
Aqilon dug tunnels, thinned his walls, let the coalition storm the city and burnt it with them
inside. He took **Perorigak**, crossed the river to **Perostro** on a night of heavy rain, and broke
Penaros. At **Avenas** the Pebelos hero **Vhomanor Hask** killed Lwrash, but the city fell. Vhomanor
retreated into the mountains to **Sorvas**. Aqilon's sappers brought the mountainside down on the
city and buried it in ice and earth. Vhomanor's corpse was dug out and sent to **Dokur**, and Pebelos
asked for terms.

On the road to Dokur Aqilon fell from his horse and broke his leg. At Dokur he was acknowledged
master of all Vahyr (**0 FC, the Foundation**), and there the leg was cut off. He came home slowly,
feverish, rumoured dead. His son **Verson II**, regent at Avolor, declared the man at the gate an
impostor and put him in a cell, where he died.

### The Vahyrian Empire (0 to 601 FC)

One continent under the Ban-Aloid. Many faiths coexisted, and the cult of Aqilon's legend served as
state religion. Unrest was endemic and purges were frequent, but roads, ports and peace made the
continent rich. The **Imperial Inland Seas Transport Guild** (IISTG) was founded.

**The First Schism (262 FC).** Principism was a swarm of sects, some worshipping one principium as a
god. The **Children of Rede** burnt people alive, following the teaching of **Grizx the Hesitant**: a
foundling of Perorigak who burnt down his orphanage, spent four thousand days in a lightless
dungeon, came out blind, saw Rede teach him that fire restores the world's balance, and preached as
a beggar until he vanished. **Poeloc III** convened the Old House, under Aos, still its patriarch, and
fixed **Early Qothism** as the official faith, with **Adswarism**, **Silihism** and **Principlurism**
tolerated. The Children of Rede were executed.

**The Salt War (596 to 601 FC).** The seas of Pyr are fresh, and salt came only from the mines of Vu.
**Verester II**, a zealot of Qoth, buried heretics alive so that Qoth might grow truth from their
error, and among them were merchants of Vu. Vu stopped the salt. The empire attacked and lost at sea,
until the Guild's engineer **Forje Nasrop** built the **semi-submerged galley**. The army landed and
Vu fell. King **Jevunavosh** was buried alive under a mound of salt in the garden of an imperial
palace at Avolor, from then on the **Salt Palace**, home of the emperors.

### The Pyrean Empire (601 to 1321 FC)

The whole of Pyr under one emperor and one church. Religion was policed, the countryside was
prosperous and pious, craft was excellent, and the clergy reached into every household.

**The First Expeditions.** The **IISTG** found the
**south vortex** (Abkelop Sortav, 688) and the **north vortex** (Loz Quintiliym, 731), built the
**submarine galley** (Arnest Goprufid, 752), went out into the cosmic sea (Kaeros Lougi, 779),
reached **Belune** one way (Josu Exculato, 806) and then there and back (Barnoix Barnutum, 833).
Trade followed, Medusosian ideas came to Pyr, and Qothist missionaries went to Belune.

**The Second Schism (1004 FC).** At a council of the Old House over **Firaperc Ban**'s thesis of
the *two faces of Qoth*, fire took the house and everyone in it. The Old House was extinct, and the
fire was read as Qoth's own verdict. Two brothers of the cadet line, **Alos and Adelim Ban-Goerim**,
fought over the inheritance, and a village priest, **Dusher Polosik**, declared the whole house
failed. Three churches came out of it with no centre: **Genesive** (Alos), **Figurative** (Adelim)
and **Principative** (Dusher) Qothism.

**Belune.** **Aqilon V** planted trading posts and sanatoria at Belune's hot springs, whose waters
cure black bile. The sick of Pyr brought plagues that nearly wiped out Belune's natives. **Polop II**
built the **Imperial Thermal Sanatorium** for his daughter **Jelebanne**, who lived out her life there
and knit the colonies into a province. In 1321 Belune became a kingdom of the empire under her line.

### The Two-Headed Empire (1321 to 1703 FC)

Two courts, at Avolor and at the Sanatorium, rivalling each other in wealth and refinement.
Qothism and Medusosianism coexisted. Poetry, embroidery, flower arrangement and ink painting
flourished, and schools and academies spread.

**The Elf and the Princess (1686 to 1703 FC).** **Beatilda**, youngest daughter of **Teosodoc II** and
granddaughter through her mother of **Old Oppretitette** (matriarch of the Red Brick House, with arms
three metres long on an ordinary body), saw the chained elf during a childhood visit. She found the
archives and understood. She planned its escape. The Red Brick House hired the **Ticklers' Guild** to
kill her, and the assassin **Jhoeland Leneu, the Seagull**, got himself hired as her guard, fell for
her and changed sides. They faked her death in a fire at the Salt Palace, freed the elf, and booked
passage on the expedition to newly found **Prunil**. The night before departure Jhoeland sold them
back. The elf returned to its cell, Jhoeland lost his tongue, and Beatilda was tortured into
confessing a coup. The emperor, preparing to storm the Guild, was murdered. The Red Brick House
purged the Ban-Aloid, and **Oppretitette became empress**. A **Red Tribunal** exiled Beatilda to an ice
dungeon on **Varam**, and days after she arrived, **Varam hatched**. **Beatildism**, the faith that
she lives on as a god in the jellyfish, has been hunted ever since and has never died.

### The Empire of the Nine Pearls (1703 to 2530 FC)

To buy legitimacy the Balnus allied with the Ticklers, loosened their grip, and **conquered worlds**:
**Prunil** (Rosena Ban-Balnus, a skeletal four-metre giant, became its queen); the three **Avorias**,
Green, Blue and Golden (Dosh Avory); **New Pyr** and **Aqilonia** (Violann III); **New Varam**
(Gereston Bolish). Nine worlds under a family that reigned more than it ruled. It was a libertine
and brilliant age, each world with its own art, and Medusosian ideas were welcome at court.

**The Assassination (2530 FC).** The Balnus grew huger, older and finally sterile, and so they
reigned for ages. **Akilon XII**, three metres tall and hugely fat, ruled 449 rounds of prosperity,
until a Blue Avorian courtesan killed him in his bed. His brother **Bemakor**, King of Belune, made
regent, accused the heir **Edilon III** and had him tried by a Red Tribunal. Edilon's brother
**Karmon**, who had sided with Bemakor, turned, broke into the tribunal, butchered the judges and
freed him. Belune lost the short war that followed. The murder was pinned on the Ticklers' Guild,
and Guild and Tribunal were driven underground. Edilon rebuilt the state on three pillars: the
**Principian Inquisition**, the **Plebeian Tribunal**, and the **Knights of the Cosmic Sea**.

### The Cosmic Empire (2530 to 3579 FC)

The IISTG's **iron submarines** opened the **Second Expansion**: 299 worlds visited, 229 held.
**Oox Ban-Balnus**, a ten-metre giant, swam to **Zuilkansia** when his submarine broke, was worshipped
as *Bigruw Trulhexiwn Kametzoriis*, the god fallen from the sky, conquered seventeen worlds as a god,
and was buried alive by the Inquisition, twice, since he dug himself out the first time. The
inquisitor **Cenzus Fogun** took twenty-five worlds, buried the unconverted alive, and raised temples
on the rubble of theirs. The linguist-explorer **Arneviz Geoant** reached the farthest worlds, brought
their relics and occult lore to the imperial family, and never returned from his last voyage.
Science and industry flourished, six Principist churches were permitted, and everything else was
punished. Education was military and uniform. Eugenics and extermination made the worlds alike.

**The Hatching (3579 FC).** The Balnus outgrew palaces and moved into **Red Pyramids**, five on Pyr and
three on Belune. **Vesifia III**, heiress of **Naabus VII**, a thousand rounds old, twenty-five metres
tall, furred, five-armed, too heavy-headed to walk, was sent to **Golden Avoria**, where the tallest
pyramid rose around her. Naabus VII sent four submarines to the sea floor to find the ruins of the
Eternal Temple and elf medicine to cure her. Three never returned. The fourth came back after ten
rounds with "an elf of the temple": small, pig-nosed, **red-skinned**. **A few days later Pyr
hatched.**

## III. After the Empire (0 AH onward)

With Pyr gone, the empire was gone. Two hundred and twenty-eight worlds fell back into kingdoms,
principalities and free cities. Of the Ban there remain ruins, pyramids, coins with a giant's face,
half-understood laws, six churches and a hundred heresies, and a rumour that the last empress still
breathes inside her unfinished pyramid on Golden Avoria.

And then **goblins** began to appear, **green**-skinned, where no archive had ever mentioned any.
Nobody knows where they come from.

The game takes place in this aftermath, some six centuries after the Hatching (see
`01_chronology.md`).
