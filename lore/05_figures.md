# Important Figures

Ordered by era. Each entry gives dates in FC, what the person did, what they were like, and **how they
are remembered**, which is what a book in the game would actually say. For Ban family relations, see
`06_lineages.md`.

---

## The fishing hut

### Hanuvesh and Hanuvela Oskavun, "the Witches of the Fishing Hut"
*b. c. −1235, Tavunesh Cove, Vu; d. −1052, killed by the black knight.*
- Twin daughters of a net-mending family of **Osk** descent ("Oskavun", "of Osk"), fisherwomen of the
  wild west coast. Hanuvesh the elder by an hour, practical and hard; Hanuvela the talker and the
  seller.
- Netted the elf in −1203, chained it in their hut, drank its blood. They lived 151 rounds without
  ageing and grew rich on "sea-physic". They killed anyone who came too close to the secret, including
  two magistrates and the white knight.
- **Remembered**: in Vu, as the *Two Sisters of Tavunesh*, a cautionary tale about greed and the sea. In
  Ban propaganda, not at all: the official legend has no witches. Beatildists call them the first jailers.

### The four knights of Ban
Sent by Baron Ansedor in −1053.
- **The white knight, Ser Aubrin Vallefroy** (b. −1085, d. −1053). A devout young noble of Kalom. First
  to find the hut; drugged with blood-wine and killed in his sleep.
- **The black knight, Ser Odemar Karst** (b. −1090, d. −1052). A landless veteran, Ansedor's best sword.
  Charmed and fed the blood, he guarded the witches for about a round; killed the blue knight. Then, in
  a moment of clarity, killed the twins, burnt them, and hanged himself. In Vunic tales he is the
  "Shadow-Lover"; in knightly romances of the Two-Headed era, a tragic hero undone by sorcery.
- **The blue knight, Ser Perrin Scalde** (b. −1080, d. −1052). Sent after the first two failed to
  return; killed by the black knight on the shingle.
- **The golden knight, Ser Gauderic Moll** (b. −1078, d. −980). The one who came back. Carried the elf to
  Ban in a salt-barrel. Rewarded with lands near Kalom and the hereditary wardenship of the elf's door
  on condition of silence. Founder of **House Moll**. In the official half-elf legend he is erased; in
  the secret archives of the Red Brick House he is "the Bringer".

## The Ban before the Parting

### Ansedor, Baron of Ban
*b. −1098; d. −702, aged 396.*
- A minor landed noble of Benepelop, proud, pious and in debt. Loved his daughter past reason.
- Received the elf (−1052), healed Hesperine, drank, became young, took Hesperine as his wife. Founded
  the family's fortune on the cordial of Ban and its influence on counsel.
- **Remembered**: officially as "the Baron", father of the princess, a wise old lord who welcomed the
  elf prince to his house. **[hidden]** He is the "elf prince" of the legend: the rejuvenated baron was
  the stranger who "came to the princess". Only the Red Brick House knew.

### Hesperine Ban, "the Princess"
*b. −1070; d. −640, aged 430.*
- Daughter, then wife, of Ansedor. Dying of the grey waste at 14, healed at 18.
- Spent more time with the Guest than anyone in history. Learned his tongue, and with her son Oudin
  wrote the **Ten Chapters** (the *Hesperine Codex*, completed −903).
- A melancholy, rigorous, brilliant woman. **[hidden]** She pitied the elf and never freed him. Her
  private notes, kept by the Red Brick House and read by Beatilda, are full of that contradiction.
- **Remembered**: as the **Princess** of the half-elf legend, mother of the dynasty, bride of an elf
  prince. Principists venerate her as the **Scribe of the Chapters**.

### Oudin Ban
*b. −1040; d. −512.* Son of Ansedor and Hesperine; co-author of the Chapters; patriarch −702 to −512.
Organised the family into offices. His death with three living sons caused **the Parting of the Houses**.

## The Eleventh Chapter and the Wars

### Aos Ban, patriarch of the Old House
*b. −88; patriarch −60 to 402; d. 402, aged 490.*
- Made patriarch at 28 of a dying house of four ancients. Ambitious and funny, a gifted forger of
  archaic hands, and a devout man who believed the religion needed teeth.
- Wrote the **Eleventh Chapter** (−41) with Aqilon. Grew immensely rich through the salt and alcohol
  monopoly and the sale of forgiveness. In old age, as patriarch still, he presided over the
  **Convocation of Avolor** (262) and fixed orthodoxy for the empire.
- **Remembered**: as a holy elder and "Restorer of the Lost Chapter", in the orthodox histories. Heretical
  histories (Adswarist, later Principative) call him "Aos the Salter" and doubt the chapter. Dusher
  Polosik called it a forgery outright; nobody could prove it.

### Aqilon I Ban-Aloid, "the Lame", Emperor of the Half-Elves
*b. −76; Warden of the Garden −39 to 0; emperor 0 to 1; d. 2 FC in the Tower of Salts.*
- Second son of Ludric Ban-Aloid. Ekklesia member from −44. Brilliant, cold, patient, vain, and a
  magnificent liar. His strategy was always to let the enemy come in and then close the door (Yr, the
  Rain Crossing).
- Co-forged the Eleventh Chapter; **[hidden]** poisoned his brother Verson (−40). Tyrant of Benepelop;
  conqueror of Vahyr; founder of the empire. Broke his leg on the way to his triumph, lost it, and was
  locked up by his son as an impostor.
- **Remembered**: everywhere, and in every version. Imperial art makes him the one-legged founder
  enthroned, the crutch as sceptre. Napelese memory makes him the burner of Yr. Principist legend makes
  him the defender of the Chapter against murderers. Beatildist texts call him the first usurper, and
  Balnus-era chronicles call him simply "the Founder", never mentioning the cell.

### Verson (I) Ban-Aloid
*b. −82; d. −40.* Elder brother of Aqilon, Ekklesia member from −55. Honest and dull. Sponsored the
Eleventh Chapter in good faith, then discovered that it was a forgery (**[hidden]**: he recognised
Aos's hand from their childhood letters) and meant to denounce it at the session. Found dead in bed on
the morning of the vote. Officially murdered by "enemies of the Chapter", which justified everything
that followed. His nephew was named after him; he is counted **Verson I**.

### Cassimir Oltenne, "the Awakened Prince"
*b. −75, Avolor; d. −31 in the Tower of Salts.*
- Son of a ropemaker of the Ropewalk. Claimed descent from the Oltenne princes who ruled Benepelop before
  the republic, hence "Prince". Had visions of the Garden from boyhood and claimed to have "woken" in it.
- A genuine mystic and a magnetic street preacher. **[hidden]** Aos's agents fed him money and crowds
  without his knowledge. He led the Garden Riots, was blamed for the killing of the five, and died in
  prison after nine rounds.
- **Remembered**: by the orthodox as a dangerous fanatic, by the poor of Avolor as a martyr (his cell
  was a secret shrine for centuries), and by the Beatildists and the Principatives as the one honest man
  in the affair.

### Lwrash Ban-Balnus, "the Red Giant"
*b. −310; d. −6 at Avenas.*
- Son of Naabus I and Ottaline. Four metres tall by 300. Violent, sullen and brave; expelled from the
  Red Brick House for killing a servant. Kept alive by a ration of blood that Aqilon obtained for him.
- Aqilon's general: retook Kalom with plague, held Yr, killed thousands. Killed in single combat on the
  causeway of Avenas by Vhomanor Hask.
- **Remembered**: in Napel and Pebelos as an ogre from a nursery tale ("Lwrash will get you"). The Balnus
  later erased him from the Register's public copies, since a giant general showed too plainly what the
  blood did.

### Vhomanor Hask, the Hero of Pebelos
*b. −44, Sorvas; d. −1 in the Burial of Sorvas.*
- Son of **Hask the Elder**, clan-chief of the Sorvan hearths. General of Pebelos. Enormously strong,
  loved by his soldiers, a mountain fighter.
- Killed Lwrash at Avenas; waged five rounds of mountain war; died when Aqilon brought the mountain down.
  His corpse was dug out and sent to Dokur to break the Hearth-Moot's will.
- **Remembered**: the national hero of Pebelos, the subject of the *Lay of the Shoulder* and of a hundred
  songs, invoked by every northern rebellion. Imperial art shows him kneeling at Aqilon's feet, which
  never happened.

### Verson II Ban-Aloid, "the Regent"
*b. −48; regent −12 to 1; emperor 1 to 96; d. 96.*
- Aqilon's son by his sister-wife Orbanne. Governed Benepelop through the wars, capably.
- **[hidden]** Recognised his father perfectly well at the gate, and chose the throne.
- **Remembered**: as the careful builder of the empire who rebuilt Yr, laid the roads and founded the
  Guild; in popular songs, as "the son who did not know his father", a byword for ingratitude.

## The Vahyrian and Pyrean Empires

### Grizx the Hesitant
*b. 198, Perorigak (foundling); vanished 247.*
- Left as a baby at the Foundling House of Perorigak, surnamed **Perorin** like all its children. Set the
  House on fire at 13 (nine children died). Spent some 4,000 days in the **Blind Cells**. Came out blind
  into the daylight.
- Had his vision of **Rede**: fire is how the world restores its balance; what burns is returned to
  chance. Preached in the streets as a beggar for 24 rounds. Called "the Hesitant" for the long silences
  before he answered anything.
- **[hidden]** Grizx never preached human sacrifice. He preached that people should let things burn
  and not rebuild. He walked into the Pereth at night in 247. His followers made the rest.
- **Remembered**: by the orthodox as the father of the burners; by the Children of Rede as their prophet;
  by the blind beggars of Penaros as a patron.

### Poeloc III Ban-Aloid, "the Convener"
*b. 165; emperor 236 to 330.* Younger brother of Poeloc II, who died young. Practical, irreligious and
orderly. Convened the Old House in 262 and made Early Qothism official "so that there will be one god
to blame". Executed the Children of Rede by fire.

### Verester II Ban-Aloid, "the Salter"
*b. 520; emperor 545 to 640.*
- A fanatical Qothist who fasted on the Silent Day and the day after it, and who spoke to the seed in
  his hand. Invented **the sowing**: burying heretics alive so Qoth might grow truth from their error.
- Buried Vunic merchants, provoked the Salt War, conquered Vu, and buried King Jevunavosh under salt in
  his own garden. He lived in the Salt Palace afterwards and slept, it was said, facing the mound.
- **Remembered**: by imperial historians as the unifier of Pyr; by Vu as the monster; by Principative
  Qothists as the proof of what a zealot on a throne does.

### Jevunavosh, last Tide-King of Vu
*b. 548; king 571 to 601; d. 601, buried alive under salt in Avolor.*
- Son of Kesunavosh. A calm, stubborn, meditative king who halted the salt trade in 594 when his
  merchants were buried, knowing it meant war.
- Won the sea-war until the semi-submerged galleys came. Sat in the Sitting Hall as Hanavun fell and let
  himself be taken without a word. Said nothing at his trial; the Stillness teaches silence. Buried under
  salt in the Garden of Lilies.
- **Remembered**: in Vu as the Silent King, whose silence under the salt still holds. Vunic custom is to
  keep silent a moment when salting food. In the empire, the Salt Palace kept his name and forgot him.

### Forje Nasrop
*b. 560, Perostro; d. 630.* Guild shipwright who designed the **semi-submerged galley** (598) after
watching river-otters swim. Rewarded with a seat on the Guild's council. Hated sailing.

### The IISTG pioneers
- **Abkelop Sortav** (650 to 712): captain who fought his way through the southern gyre and first
  sighted the **Southern Throat** (688). Charted the gyre; lost two ships doing it.
- **Loz Quintiliym** (695 to 760): cold-water captain from Dokur who reached the **Northern Throat**
  (731); pioneered ice-sheathed hulls.
- **Arnest Goprufid** (715 to 790): engineer of the **submarine galley** (752). Went half-deaf from
  pressure trials.
- **Kaeros Lougi** (748 to 812): first human to ride a vortex wall out into the **cosmic sea** (779) and
  drop back down the eye. His account of the embers and the endless water is the most copied text of
  the Pyrean era.
- **Josu Exculato** (770 to c. 811): reached **Belune** (806) with five crew and could not return.
  Lived among the Belune tribes; left a journal in a cairn.
- **Barnoix Barnutum** (790 to 860): the first **round trip** to Belune (833), with Josu's journal as his
  proof. Founder of the **Tables of Barnutum**, the star-charts of the cosmic sea.

### Firaperc Ban
*b. 790; d. 1004 in the Burning Council.* Scholar of the Old House, son of Hugon. Proposed the
**ontological duality of Qoth**: Qoth has two faces, a principium among principia (the seventh answer) and
an omnipotent maker (the creator of worlds). A council was called to judge the thesis, and the house
burned during it. **[hidden]** The fire started in the archive-stove of the lower library and was an
accident. Firaperc's thesis survives in fragments quoted by those who refuted it.

### Alos and Adelim Ban-Goerim
- **Alos** (930 to 1150): the elder; stern and legalistic. Claimed the Old House's inheritance as
  senior surviving male of the blood; founded **Genesive Qothism** at Avolor.
- **Adelim** (945 to 1162): the younger; poet and mystic. Held that the fire had judged Firaperc's
  *form*, not his insight; founded **Figurative Qothism** at Perorigak.
- The brothers did not speak for 140 rounds. Each died believing the other damned.

### Dusher Polosik
*b. 960, Lusk; d. 1060.* A village priest, son of a Napelese ferryman, not a Ban and never given blood.
Declared the Old House's failure a judgment on the whole hierarchy, preached a plain faith (no salt
monopoly, no forgiveness for gold, no intermediaries), and founded the **Plain Church** of **Principative
Qothism** (1009). Called the Eleventh Chapter "a merchant's chapter". Survived three attempts on his life.

### Aqilon V Ban-Aloid, "of the Springs"
*b. 990; emperor 1058 to 1131.* Founded the trading posts and sanatoria of Belune; unwittingly caused the
Exchange Plagues. Bathed in the springs every round and believed they would make him immortal.

### Polop II Ban-Aloid, "the Grieving"
*b. 1100; emperor 1172 to 1262.* Built the Imperial Thermal Sanatorium in 1212 for his daughter
Jelebanne. Visited her every round until he died.

### Jelebanne Ban-Aloid, Queen of Belune
*b. 1195; at the Sanatorium from 1212; queen 1321 to 1392; d. 1392.*
- Sent to Belune at 17 with black bile corruption. The springs held it at bay but never cured it. She
  could never leave.
- Turned exile into a work: roads, the **Belune Code**, the Sanatorium Chancery, the protection (too
  late) of the native Remnant. Made Belune a kingdom of the empire in 1321.
- Wrote poetry and painted in ink; the founder of the Belunese courtly arts.
- **Remembered**: as "the Queen in the Steam", a patient, gentle saint-administrator. Medusosians honour
  her because she protected their shrines.

## The Two-Headed Empire and the Coup

### Teosodoc II Ban-Aloid
*b. 1575; emperor 1640 to 1703; assassinated 1703.* A scholarly, affectionate, weak emperor, dominated by
his Balnus in-laws. When he learned his daughter lived and was in the Guild's hands, he tried to raise the
Salt Guard against the Ticklers' Guild. He was killed in his bath the night before.

### Beatilda Ban-Aloid, "the Princess of Varam"
*b. 1683; "died" 1702; exiled 1703 to Varam, which hatched days later.*
- Youngest daughter of Teosodoc II and Empress Clemence Ban-Balnus. Baptised at the Red Brick House on her
  1100th day (1686). Visited her grandmother Oppretitette often, and loved her.
- About 1697, following a servant down a stair she should not have taken, she saw the Guest in the Well-Room.
  She spent three rounds in the family archives, reading Hesperine's notes and the Register, until she
  understood. Guilt made her plan his escape.
- Won over her would-be assassin, faked her death, freed the elf, and was betrayed on the eve of flight.
  Tortured by the Guild into "confessing" a coup. Condemned by a Red Tribunal to the ice of Vaur. Days
  later Varam hatched.
- Brave, clever, sheltered, stubborn, often naïve about the people she trusted.
- **Remembered**: officially as a conspirator and parricide who "died in the fire of 1702", then was
  quietly written out. **Beatildism** makes her the rightful empress, reborn as the Varam jellyfish, the
  one Ban who tried to free the Well. Romances of the Nine Pearls era, which the censors tolerated, made her
  a tragic lover of the Seagull.

### Jhoeland Leneu, "the Seagull"
*b. 1672, Perostro; d. c. 1760.*
- Son of a net-mender of the Perostro docks; an assassin of the Ticklers' Guild, known for approaching
  targets over the water and for laughing. Hired as Beatilda's guard to kill her; changed sides; loved her.
- On the eve of sailing, betrayed her back to the Guild. **[hidden]** The Guild had taken his mother and
  sister, and he bought their lives with hers. His tongue was cut out, and he served the Guild as a
  mute enforcer for another fifty rounds.
- **Remembered**: as the archetypal traitor ("a seagull's kiss"); in Beatildist tradition as Judas and,
  in a minority heresy, as a penitent saint.

### Old Oppretitette Ban-Balnus, Matriarch and Empress
*b. 480 FC; matriarch 1011 to 1703; empress 1703 to 2081; d. 2081, aged 1,601.*
- Daughter of Ottaline and Naabus III, born when her mother was nearly a thousand. By 1700 her body was
  ordinary-sized but her **arms were three metres long**; she could reach across the Well-Room without rising.
- Shrewd, patient, affectionate to her grandchildren, and absolutely ruthless for the house. Ordered her
  granddaughter's death, purged the Ban-Aloid, took the throne. Then relaxed the empire's grip and sent it
  out to conquer, which made her reign popular.
- **Remembered**: as the "Long-Armed Empress" who founded the Nine Pearls, in statues with arms that reach
  to the base; in Beatildist texts as the Grandmother, the devourer.

### Clemence Ban-Balnus, Empress-consort
*b. 1600; d. 1690.* Daughter of Oppretitette, married to Teosodoc II in 1660 in one of the rare unions
between the houses. Mother of Beatilda. Wasted and died at 90 living on the Aloid diet at the Salt Palace:
a Balnus off the blood sickens.

### Akilon VIII Ban-Balnus
*b. 1010; d. 1703.* Son of Oppretitette, **Keeper of the Well** for four centuries. Killed at the
Well-Room door by Jhoeland on the night the elf was freed. His death is why the Red Brick House never forgave.

## The Nine Pearls

### Rosena Ban-Balnus, Queen of Prunil
*b. 1000; queen 1722 to 2390; d. 2390.* Niece of Oppretitette. Four metres tall and skeletally thin.
Conquered Prunil (1705 to 1722) with extreme brutality (the Gorges). As queen she made it the gold-purse of
the empire, and lived in a tower built to her shape.

### Dosh Avory
*b. 1695, Perostro; d. 1779.* Not a Ban. An IISTG captain turned conqueror; took **Green, Blue and Golden
Avoria** (1731 to 1771) with small forces and alliances with native rivals. A jovial, practical man who
named three worlds after himself. His descendants, **House Avory**, governed the Avorias until 2533.

### Violann III Ban-Balnus
*b. 1650; d. 3020.* Daughter of Rosena. Conquered **New Pyr** and **Aqilonia** (1780 to 1799) at her mother's
orders, "young" at 130. Governor of New Pyr for centuries; late in life one of the eldest of the family.

### Gereston Bolish
*b. 1782, Dokur; d. 1850.* A Pebelene explorer who found **New Varam** (1811) on a voyage everyone expected
him not to survive, and conquered it (1815 to 1829). A melancholy man who wrote the *Northern Letters*.

### Akilon XII Ban-Balnus
*b. 1300; emperor 2081 to 2530; murdered 2530.* Son of Oppretitette. Three metres tall, enormously fat, genial,
lazy, and lucky. His 449-round reign was the high summer of the Nine Pearls: the worlds governed themselves
and paid tribute, and the capital went to the opera. Killed in his bed by **Ysmé Varrocq**, a courtesan of
Blue Avoria. **[hidden]** Ysmé was paid by Bemakor through a Tickler cell.

## The Cosmic Empire

### Bemakor Ban-Balnus, King of Belune
*b. 1380; king 1704 to 2532; killed 2532 at the Causeway of Oranse.* Brother of Akilon XII. Given Belune
after the Red Night, married to Loquine of the murdered Aloid line. Regent after the assassination, he
accused his nephew Edilon, lost the War of the Regency, and died on the causeway.

### Edilon III Ban-Balnus
*b. 2150; emperor 2530 to 2632; d. 2632.* Accused of his father's murder, tried, rescued by his brother.
Blamed the empire's softness, and built the Inquisition, the Plebeian Tribunal and the Knights. Hard,
sincere, humourless, and a believer. Launched the second expansion.

### Karmon Ban-Balnus
*b. 2170; d. 2780.* Brother of Edilon. Sided with Bemakor, then broke into the Red Tribunal and killed the
judges to save his brother. First **Grand Master of the Knights of the Cosmic Sea** (2534 to 2780).
Father of Naabus VI.

### Oox Ban-Balnus, "Bigruw Trulhexiwn Kametzoriis"
*b. 1750; conquests 2566 to 2641; buried alive 2645.*
- Son of Bemakor and Loquine. Sent into the Knights after his father's defeat, to redeem the name. Ten
  metres tall, skin like hide, prodigiously strong.
- In 2561 his submarine broke apart in the cosmic sea. He swam, for an unknown number of days, to the
  vortex of **Zuilkansia** and climbed down its wall. The tribes found him at the crater and worshipped
  him as **the god fallen from the sky**.
- In 2566 he seized the rescue submarine and, with a fanatical following, conquered seventeen worlds as
  a living god. The Inquisition took him on Trulhex in 2644 and buried him alive at Ossomire. He clawed
  his way out, and they buried him again, deeper, under a stone.
- **Remembered**: as a god on seventeen worlds (**the Faith of the Fallen God**). As a heretic and
  traitor by the Inquisition. In imperial family lore, as the proof that the blood makes gods of monsters.

### Cenzus Fogun, Inquisitor
*b. 2572; conquests 2598 to 2650; d. 2661.* A Genesive inquisitor of humble birth from Napel. Conquered
twenty-five worlds for the faith: mass conversions, mass burials of the unconverted, and great temples
raised on the rubble of native shrines. Captor of Oox. Died of a fever on Salsuge, among the pilgrims'
seed-graves. **Remembered**: as a saint by the Genesive church, and on his twenty-five worlds as a demon.

### Arneviz Geoant
*b. 2790; voyages 2815 to 2868; vanished 2871.*
- IISTG explorer and linguist, the son of a Perorigak schoolmaster. He reached the farthest worlds of
  the empire and learned languages in days.
- Brought relics and tales to court, and with them the occult: the speaking stones of Ysthane, hollow-layer
  cults, "elf-science". Became advisor to Naabus VI and Diferon III. Sailed in 2871 for a world beyond
  the star-tables and never returned.
- **Remembered**: as the great explorer; as a corrupter of the dynasty (Inquisition); as a seer who
  found the way out (esoteric circles, who expect him back).

### Naabus VII Ban-Balnus
*b. 2410; emperor 3181 to 3579; d. 3579 in the Hatching.* The last emperor. Learned, obsessive, raised
on Arneviz's lore. Sent the Deep Expeditions (3569) to find elf medicine for his daughter in the ruins
of the Eternal Temple. The *Patience of Rhesis* came back after ten rounds. Pyr hatched a few days later.

### Vesifia III Ban-Balnus, "the Last Empress"
*b. 2579, the last child born to the Ban; exiled to Golden Avoria 3420; fate unknown.*
- Heiress of Naabus VII. She never stopped growing: by 1,000 rounds, twenty-five metres, furred all over,
  five arms with hands of three to eleven fingers, and a head too heavy for her to walk.
- Exiled to Golden Avoria in 3420, where the **Great Red Pyramid** was built around her.
- **[hidden]** She was alive at the Hatching. Nobody knows how long she lived after, fed by a dwindling
  household, with no blood arriving from Pyr. See `12_secrets_and_mysteries.md`.
- **Remembered**: as the **Last Empress**, rumoured still alive inside her pyramid; the subject of cults,
  pilgrimages and treasure-hunts.

### Captain Idrenne Sauval of the *Patience of Rhesis*
*b. 3530; returned 3579; d. 3579.* Commander of the one deep ship that came back. Her log (partly
recovered from Belune's copies of the last dispatches) describes the sand-floor, the ruins, and the
capture of "an elf of the temple": small, red-skinned, pig-nosed, clawed, "speaking a click-language, and
unafraid". She died with Pyr.

## Other figures

### Nemeth Oraun, "the Descended", prophet of Belune
*Lived c. −900 FC (Belunese reckoning unclear).* A Belunese herder who went down the Gullet after a
lost goat, walked for days to the hollow layer, lived among the dwarfs of **Oggerund**, and came back
with their faith: the embryo gathers the souls of the dead into a god. Founder of **Medusosianism**
among humans.

### Ysmé Varrocq
*b. 2505, Blue Avoria; executed 2530.* Courtesan and tide-opera singer, favourite of Akilon XII, whom she
killed with a hairpin through the ear. Said at her execution only: "He was heavy." Blue Avorian songs
make her a heroine.

### Maelis Tourbe
*b. 2490; d. 2566.* IISTG engineer of the iron submarine (2537) at Calvassa's forges. Blind in one eye
from a rivet.

### Grand Inquisitor Amaury Chastel
*b. 2505; d. 2580.* First head of the Principian Inquisition (2533 to 2580); Genesive. Author of the
*Manual of Sowing*, the Inquisition's procedure for burials.
