# I. Of Anatomy

Everything a character can do is read, directly or at one remove, off the constitution of its body.
The body is therefore the first subject, and the longest.

## 1. The three degrees of the frame

The frame is articulated in three descending degrees.

- A **region** is a major division of the body — the encephalon, the trunk. It is the coarsest unit
  and the one a blow is aimed at.
- An **organ** belongs to exactly one region.
- An **organ part** belongs to exactly one organ, and is the smallest unit that bears a score. An
  organ of a single part carries the same identifier as the part beneath it; a paired organ, such as
  the eyes, has two.

Score accumulates upward and is never held at more than one degree: **the score of an organ is the
sum of its parts, and the score of a region the sum of its organs.** Nothing is stored at the two
upper degrees. The same holds of the maxima, so a region's ceiling is the sum of every ceiling
beneath it.

An organ part's ceiling is **five** unless its own kind declares otherwise, and a species may
declare otherwise again (§4). Its score is confined to the interval from nought to that ceiling.

## 2. The human frame

Five regions, twenty-two organs, twenty-eight organ parts.

| Region | Ceiling | Organs (organ parts, ceiling each) |
|---|---|---|
| **Encephalon** | 23 | anamnesis 5 · cerebellum 5 · cerebrum 5 · hippocampus 5 · pineal gland 3 |
| **Visage** | 22 | eyes (left 3, right 3) · ears (left 3, right 3) · nose 3 · tongue 5 · teeth 2 |
| **Trunk** | 28 | backbone 4 · heart 5 · viscera 5 · hepar 3 · spleen 3 · paunch 3 · pulmones 3 · genitories 2 |
| **Upper limbs** | 10 | arms (left 2, right 2) · hands (left 3, right 3) |
| **Lower limbs** | 10 | legs (left 2, right 2) · feet (left 3, right 3) |

Each organ is the seat of a family of modi mentis and the source of one or more derived measures.
(The body panel's own account of an organ speaks of its **disciplines**; it means exactly this — the
modi mentis that draw upon that organ.) Taken in order:

**Of the encephalon.** The *anamnesis* governs the persistence of what is learned; it sizes the
residual memory and the store of routines. The *cerebellum* governs practised movement and the
acquisition of dexterity under duress; it sizes procedural memory and the readiness to learn a
fighting skill in the midst of a fight. The *cerebrum* is the organ of the understanding, and sizes
semantic memory. The *hippocampus* registers what the senses convey inward, and sizes sensory
memory. The *pineal gland* is the organ of inward regard: it sets the price in experience of every
advance in every modus mentis, and the composure by which a fallen body regains its feet.

**Of the visage.** The *eyes* mediate perception at a distance, and set the reach of a ranged blow.
The *ears* govern audition and the promptitude of answer, whence the order of a combat's turns. The
*nose* is the medium of caution: it discerns humors and steers a party clear of what waits ahead.
The *tongue* is the organ of articulate speech and the measure of fluency. The *teeth* reduce the
harder aliment and deliver the rending bite; they are little called upon by the trained modi mentis.

**Of the trunk.** The *backbone* is the axial column: upon it depend what may be carried and how
much may be attempted in a turn. The *heart* is the index of the constitution's warmth — its span of
days, its residual youth, and the number of companions it can sustain. The *viscera* are the seat of
fortitude: they govern how swiftly a wound closes and how far a roll may be tempered by the humors.
The *hepar*, *spleen*, *paunch* and *pulmones* are the four **sanguific** organs and are treated
apart, in [Chapter III](03-humors.md); each renders humors into its own queue in proportion to its
condition. The *genitories* determine the sex and lend the raw vigour behind a blow; they have no
further office in the working of the frame.

**Of the limbs.** The *arms* are the organs of gross force, the *hands* of manual dexterity — the
latter setting how much potency may be drawn from an implement. The *legs* mediate celerity, and the
*feet* stance and endurance, whence sure footing on treacherous ground and the dice of flight.

## 3. The bestial frame

A second anatomy exists, of four regions, and the beasts are constituted upon it.

| Region | Organs |
|---|---|
| **Encephalon** | as the human: anamnesis · cerebellum · cerebrum · hippocampus · pineal gland |
| **Muzzle** | eyes · ears · snout · tongue · fangs |
| **Trunk** | backbone · heart · pulmones · viscera · paunch · hepar · spleen |
| **Limbs** | legs (fore and hind, left and right) · claws (fore and hind, left and right) |

Two consequences are easy to overlook. The bestial trunk has **no genitories**, so every measure
drawn from that organ is simply absent from a beast. And the identifiers are shared wherever the
anatomy is shared: the bestial legs answer to *legs* exactly as the human legs do, and both trunks
to *trunk*. A doctrine that reasons by the name of the organ is therefore correct across both
frames; one that reasons by its constitution is not.

The *snout* stands to the beast as the nose to man, of far greater development; the *fangs* as the
teeth, and as its principal armament.

## 4. Species

A **species** is not an anatomy. It names an anatomy and may then revise the ceilings of individual
organ parts. Wolf, bear, boar, dog, cat and fox all stand upon the bestial anatomy and differ only
in these ceilings — a wolf's fangs reach five where another creature's reach less, its forelegs
five, its paunch three, its pineal gland two. Man is the sole species upon the human anatomy.

Whence the rule that governs every formula in this manual: **a measure is scaled against the body's
own maximum, not against a constant.** A creature whose heart tops out lower than man's still
reaches the full span its heart allows; the bar it fills is shorter, not unfillable.

## 5. The three capabilities

Anatomy alone does not settle what a body may attempt. A modus mentis naming *fangs* is out of a
man's reach because he has none, and that requires no declaration. But a wolf owns a tongue and a
cerebrum, and nothing in its constitution forbids it rhetoric or the picking of a lock. Something
must be said.

Three capabilities are therefore declared of each anatomy:

- **Speech** — to hold a conversation with a person: language, not merely voice. A beast may howl
  and snarl, for those modi mentis claim no speech; it can never open a conversation.
- **Handcraft** — fine manipulation: implements, locks, knots, carrying, cutting. What distinguishes
  a hand from a paw.
- **Abstraction** — letters, number, law, coin, doctrine: thought resting upon human institutions
  rather than upon the senses. A beast may track, remember and scheme; it cannot reckon or read a
  charter.

The human anatomy has all three. The bestial anatomy has none.

The declaration runs in one direction only: **an anatomy declares what it has, and a modus mentis or
an act declares what it requires.** What requires nothing — the common case — is open to every
frame. A body may hold a modus mentis when it owns every organ the modus mentis names *and* has
every capability the modus mentis requires; failing either, the modus mentis is refused outright
rather than held uselessly.

## 6. The derived measures

A **derived measure** is computed from exactly one source — an organ part, an organ, or a region —
by a formula of its own. The measures are not a fixed list bolted to the anatomy; every measure
declares its own source and is found by search, so a body has whichever measures its organs admit
and no others.

Where a measure's source is absent from a frame, the measure is unusable and reads at its **worst
value** — which for most is a floor, and for a duration or a price is a ceiling.

### From the regions

| Measure | Source | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Noetic Points | encephalon | ⌈(score + 3) ÷ 3⌉, never below 1 |
| Working Memory | encephalon | score + 2, bounded 1–25 |
| Countenance | visage | score ÷ 5, in dice — a visage at its ceiling of 22 is worth 4 |
| Health Points | trunk | ⌈(score + 3) ÷ 3⌉, never below 1 |
| Natural Defence | lower limbs | score ÷ 3, in dice |
| Max Travel Distance | lower limbs | from 4.0 units at nought to 14.8 at the region's ceiling, linearly |

### From the organs

|---|---|---|

| Routine Queue | anamnesis | score × 10, never below 10 |

The four sanguific organs each yield four measures more, being the proportions in which they render
the four cardinal humors. These are given in [Chapter III](03-humors.md).

### The contribution to a ceiling

Every organ and every region additionally bears one measure of a distinct kind: its **contribution
to the maximum level of a modus mentis**. An organ contributes from nought to **+3**, a region from
nought to **+6**, scaled linearly against the source's own maximum. These are what set how far any
modus mentis may be raised, and are treated in [Chapter II](02-memory.md).

## 7. Wounds

A wound is of a **kind** — the catalogue entry, which is fixed and shared — and an **instance**,
which is one injury upon one body. Only the instance knows when it was taken.

A wound names what it afflicts: an organ part, an organ, a whole region, or nothing in particular (a
**wildcard**, being the generic contusion, cut and puncture that a glancing blow leaves). Which of
the three generic wounds a near-miss leaves depends on the shape of the harm — an edge cuts, a point
punctures, a blunt weight contends — so a sword's graze and a mace's are not the same graze.

Three degrees of handicap:

- **Low** — no penalty to any score. It costs one point of health and nothing else.
- **Medium** — subtracts one from the effective score of what it afflicts. Negative scores are
  possible, and every measure drawn from that source falls accordingly.
- **High** — disables what it afflicts entirely. Every measure drawn from it collapses to its worst
  value.

Health is not a pool that is spent: **current health is the maximum less the count of wounds
borne.** A body dies when the count reaches the maximum.

Two measures deliberately pass through the wound-aware path rather than round it, so that damage to
the organ is felt in kind. A medium wound to the **heart** takes real days off a life, and a high
one collapses the span to its floor — which will kill outright anyone already older than that. A
wound to the **viscera** lengthens the recovery of every other wound the body bears.

**Healing.** Only Low and Medium wounds ever close; a High wound is permanent. A wound closes after
the number of days the viscera dictate, counted from the day it was taken. Progress is not ticked
forward — it is derived from the calendar whenever it is asked for, and is therefore always correct.

A wound instance bearing **no day of infliction is historical**: the scars an NPC carries out of its
own past, the injuries a protagonist begins with. Historical wounds never heal.

## 8. Age and the term of life

The world's calendar counts **days**, and the day is the unit every span is expressed in. Age is
never stored. Each body is stamped at its making with the reading of the calendar at its birth, and
its age is thereafter the difference between that reading and the present — so every creature ages
as the calendar advances, with nothing to keep in step.

A body created already aged is capped at nine tenths of its own term, so that no one is born past
their death.

The term itself is the heart's measure. A body dies of age once its age reaches that term.

## 9. The composition of a protagonist

A protagonist is composed by distributing a budget of **twenty-five points** across the organ parts
of the human frame. Every part begins at **one**; the budget buys the excess, and no part may be
raised past its own ceiling. Since the twenty-eight parts admit sixty-five points of excess between
them, the budget purchases rather less than half of what a body could be — the composition is a
matter of what to forgo.

Two things are settled the moment composition ends and cannot be revisited by any later
distribution:

- **The memory is built** to the capacities the final scores dictate (Chapter II).
- **The humor queues are filled** by secretion from the final scores of the four sanguific organs,
  with black bile withheld, and a fraction of the slots equal to the youthfulness measure is
  overwritten with the humor of youth (Chapter III).

A protagonist begins holding exactly one modus mentis, that of childhood recollection, at the first
level; everything else is learned in play.
