# X. Crafting

Crafting turns items into materials at a station, and materials back into items. This chapter gives the rules for materials, stations, trades, and the rolls that decide whether a thing is made.

**In short**

1. A **trade** is a modus mentis (a learned skill; see [Chapter II](02-memory.md)) that works one material, at one kind of station, with one implement.
2. Setting to work at a station is an act of difficulty 2 that requires an implement.
3. At the station, breaking an item down returns its units of the station's material, with no roll.
4. Making an item costs the units it holds and is rolled on its own: every trade held for the station, every implement of the station carried, and the hands' Handiwork add dice.
5. Material left over when the work ends is thrown away.
6. A station in a workshop with a master needs the master's leave, asked once in conversation and kept for good.

## 1. Materials

A **material** is what an item is made of, counted in **units**.

**Rules**

1. There are 11 materials: ore, metal, wood, fibre, cloth, hide, leather, clay, fat, bone and stone.
2. Every item that is not consumed is made of one or several materials. A knife is metal and wood, a tunic is cloth, a horseshoe is metal.
3. A consumable may be made of none. A loaf of bread or an apple holds nothing a station can use.
4. An item holds a number of units of **each** of its materials, set by its burden (how much carrying capacity it uses; see [Chapter IV §1](04-items.md#1-burden)).
5. The number doubles at each burden step, and is the same for every material in the item. A knife (light) holds 2 units of metal and 2 of wood.
6. Making an item costs exactly what breaking it down returns, so the two directions always agree.

| Burden | Insignificant | Light | Medium | Heavy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Units of each material | 1 | 2 | 4 | 8 |

### Refined materials

**Rules**

1. Three materials are **refined** from a raw one: ore becomes metal, fibre becomes cloth, hide becomes leather.
2. Each refining is done at a station that works the raw material (§2).
3. Refining is a single step. A refined material is never refined again.

## 2. Stations

A **station** is a kind of point of interest (an object in a place that can be acted on) where crafting is done.

**Rules**

1. Every station works exactly one material.
2. Breaking an item down at a station returns that material and no other.
3. Making an item at a station is paid for in that material.
4. Several stations may work the same material (the loom and the spinning wheel both work fibre), but no station works two.
5. A workshop often holds more than one station: a forge has a hearth and an anvil, a weaver's shop has a loom and a cutting table.

| Material | Worked at |
|---|---|
| Ore | the forge hearth |
| Metal | the anvil, the goldsmith's bench |
| Wood | the workbench |
| Fibre | the loom, the spinning wheel |
| Cloth | the cutting table |
| Hide | the tanning pit |
| Leather | the leather bench, the writing desk |
| Clay | the potter's wheel |
| Fat | the tallow vat |
| Bone | the carving bench |
| Stone | the mason's banker |

## 3. Trades

A **trade** is a modus mentis with the **Craft** function (the role that lets it work a station).

**Rules**

1. A trade names one kind of **station**, and so one material.
2. A trade names one **implement** (the item it works with).
3. A trade names the kinds of item it makes: from 1 to 5.
4. No two trades share both a station and an implement. Two trades may work at the anvil, and two may use shears, but not two at the anvil with the hammer.
5. Reason: this lets the implement in hand name the trade at work (§5).
6. Every trade requires the use of hands, so no beast can hold one.
7. A trade is kept in procedural memory.
8. A trade has three functions: Craft, Action and **Will** (a want the modus mentis pursues on its own; see [Chapter II §7](02-memory.md#7-what-the-modi-mentis-are-for)).

### What a trade wants

As a will, a trade wants five things, in this order of preference:

1. To set to work at its station, as itself.
2. To take up something made of its material: gathered, picked up, cut from a carcass, mined, felled or dug.
3. To buy something made of its material from someone who sells it.
4. To take up its implement.
5. To study, by examining or contemplating, whatever teaches it.

**Rules**

1. As with every will, the nearest of these wants is pursued.
2. Between wants equally near, the one higher in the list is pursued.
3. Wants 2 and 3 are broad, because wood and fibre are found in most places.

## 4. A master's leave

A **master** is the owner of highest authority of a workshop who is alive and can speak.

### When leave is needed

**Rules**

1. A station inside a workshop that has a master may be used lawfully only with the master's leave.
2. A station on the workshop's entry floor (protected ground; see [Chapter VI §7](06-narration.md#7-crime-and-being-seen)) may be set to work without leave, but doing so is a crime, like any other act of taking on protected ground.
3. A station elsewhere in the workshop cannot be used at all without leave.
4. A station that no one owns (an anvil among ruins, an anvil in a street, a tanning pit in a public quarter) can be used by anyone, and no leave is needed.

### Asking for leave

**Rules**

1. Leave is asked in conversation with the master.
2. Only a character holding a trade whose station stands in that workshop can ask.
3. The conversation is a **matter of consequence**, one of the four difficulty levels of a conversation (see [Chapter VII §2](07-social.md#2-the-conversation)).
4. If the conversation succeeds, leave is given. If it fails, nothing is given, and the character may ask again.

### What leave gives

**Rules**

1. Leave, once given, is kept for good.
2. It covers every station of the workshop.
3. It is remembered for that location on every later visit.
4. It makes the character's presence in the workshop no longer trespass (see [Chapter VI §7](06-narration.md#7-crime-and-being-seen)).
5. Items on the entry floor become free to take, and taking them is no crime.
6. Items in the private rooms behind still belong to the master, and taking them is still theft.

### Leave and a trade's will

**Rules**

1. Asking leave is a step toward working, never a want of the trade itself.
2. A trade's will counts on the leave a conversation would give, and plans through it: ask the master, then set to work.
3. A will never plans to work a station without leave, nor to take up an implement to do so.
4. The conversation is carried by whichever speaking modus mentis is judged best suited, so a trade with no speech of its own can still get into a master's workshop.

## 5. Setting to work

**Setting to work** is the act that opens a station for crafting. It is an act like any other in narration (see [Chapter VI](06-narration.md)).

**Rules**

1. Setting to work is an act of **difficulty 2**: the roll needs at least 2 sixes.
2. It is offered once for each trade the character holds for that station, and only where the station may be used (§4).
3. It **requires an implement** combined with the act (see [Chapter IV §7](04-items.md#7-implements)).
4. The trade's own implement is accepted without question.
5. Any other implement is judged like every substitute: whether it could do the work of the implement wanted, and whether the character's Tool Usage Proficiency is high enough for that fit. A smith may try the trade with an axe.
6. The implement names the trade. If it is the implement of one of the character's trades for that station, that trade is the one at work, whichever was proposed. Otherwise the proposed trade is at work.
7. The roll uses the one trade at work and the one implement combined. Handiwork does not apply.
8. Success opens the station (§6) and teaches the trade at work.
9. Failure costs one of three penalties, drawn at random (see [Chapter VI §8](06-narration.md#8-failure)): a Scrape wound (a mishap wound; see [Chapter I §7](01-anatomy.md#7-wounds)), the period of the day advancing by one, or 1 extra noetic point (a point of the phase's thinking budget).

## 6. At the station

Once at work, the character can break items down and make items, as often as it likes. None of this takes time.

### Breaking down

**Rules**

1. Any carried item that holds the station's material can be destroyed.
2. Its units of that material are added to the material in hand.
3. Only the station's material is kept. Every other material in the item is lost: a knife broken down at the anvil gives its metal, and its handle is gone.
4. A container must be empty to be broken down.
5. Breaking down is never rolled.

### Making

**Rules**

1. Any item the trade makes can be attempted.
2. It costs, in the station's material, the units the made item holds (§1).
3. Nothing is attempted, and nothing spent, if too little material is in hand or if the made item could not be placed in the pack.
4. The material is paid when the dice are rolled, whether the attempt succeeds or not.
5. On a success the item goes into the pack. On a failure the material is lost and nothing is made.

### Material in hand

**Rules**

1. There is no store of material.
2. Whatever remains in hand when the work ends is thrown away.
3. The amount of material a character can bring to a station is therefore limited by what it can carry (see [Chapter IV §1](04-items.md#1-burden)).

### The making roll

Each attempt to make an item is rolled on its own.

**Rules**

1. The roll succeeds when the number of sixes is at least the difficulty, as with every roll in the game (see [Chapter VI](06-narration.md)).
2. Before the result is accepted, humors may be spent to change dice, as on any roll (see [Chapter III §4](03-humors.md#4-transmuting-virtues)).
3. The number of humors that can be spent is the viscera score (an organ score; its Humor Modifier Limit, see [Chapter I §6](01-anatomy.md#6-derived-values)), never less than 1.
4. The dice are the sum of three sources, with a minimum of 1 die.
5. **Trades**: the level of **every** trade the character holds for this kind of station, not only the one that set to work, each at the level its wounds leave it (see [Chapter II §3](02-memory.md#3-level-and-maximum-level)). A smith who knows two trades of the anvil works it with both.
6. **Implements**: the level of use of every implement of this station's trades that the character carries, whichever trade it belongs to and whether or not that trade is held. Each kind of implement counts once, using the best one carried.
7. **Handiwork**: 0 to 3 dice, a derived value of the hands' score (see [Chapter I §6](01-anatomy.md#6-derived-values)). Hands disabled by a High wound give 0.
8. The difficulty is set by the made item's reference price (its value in copper; see [Chapter IV §10](04-items.md#10-coin)), which measures the skill an item holds beyond its material.

| Hands score | 0–1 | 2–3 | 4–5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Handiwork dice | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |

| Reference price | up to 7 | 8 to 13 | 14 to 22 | above 22 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sixes needed | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |

**Example.** A character at an anvil holds one anvil trade at level 2, carries that trade's implement at level of use 3, and has a hands score of 4 (Handiwork 2). The pool is 2 + 3 + 2 = 7 dice. An item with a reference price of 16 needs 3 sixes.

## 7. The crafting routine

A **routine** is a remembered way straight into a spot in a known place (see [Chapter IX](09-routines.md)). Setting to work successfully teaches a **Craft** routine.

**Rules**

1. A Craft routine records the station, the trade, and the implement used, if that was not the trade's own.
2. It is learned wherever the character is allowed to stand: on open ground, or in a workshop whose master has given leave.
3. Walking it sets the character to work at the station directly, with no act rolled.
4. It cannot be walked when the station is gone.
5. It cannot be walked when the trade is no longer held.
6. It cannot be walked when the implement is not carried: the trade's own, or the one it was learned with.
7. It cannot be walked when the station may no longer be used.
8. It cannot be walked when an enemy is in the area.
9. Ending the work returns the character to the place.

## Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Breaking down | Destroying a carried item at a station for its units of the station's material. |
| Craft function | The role of a modus mentis that makes it a trade. |
| Difficulty | The number of sixes a roll needs to succeed. |
| Handiwork | Dice the hands add to every item made: 0 to 3. |
| Implement | A tool combined with an act; a trade names one. |
| Level of use | The bonus dice an implement adds. |
| Master | A workshop's living, speaking owner of highest authority. |
| Material | One of 11 substances an item is made of, counted in units. |
| Modus mentis | A learned skill (plural modi mentis). |
| Noetic point | A point of a narration phase's budget for thinking and acting. |
| Refined material | Metal, cloth or leather: made from ore, fibre or hide. |
| Reference price | An item's value in copper; sets the difficulty of making it. |
| Routine | A remembered way straight into a spot in a known place. |
| Station | A point of interest that works one material. |
| Trade | A modus mentis that works one station with one implement. |
| Unit | One measure of a material; an item holds 1, 2, 4 or 8 of each. |
| Will | A want a modus mentis pursues on its own. |
