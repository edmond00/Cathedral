# X. Of Crafting

Crafting is the knowledge of a **trade**: one manner of working, practised at one kind of
**station**, with one **implement**, upon one **material**. A trade is a modus mentis (see
[II](02-memory.md)), and everything a body may make it makes through one.

The work has two halves and they are settled separately. **Setting to work** at a station is an act
like any other ([VI](06-narration.md)): it is rolled, it may fail, and it teaches. **The work itself**
is rolled for nothing and costs no time: whatever is taken apart and whatever is made at the station
is decided by what the body carries and what the trade knows, and by nothing else.

## 1. Materials

Every item is made of nought, one or several **materials**, of which there are eleven: ore, metal,
wood, fibre, cloth, hide, leather, clay, fat, bone and stone. A knife is of metal and wood, a tunic of
cloth, a horseshoe of metal; a loaf of bread, a feather or a letter is of nothing a station can use.

**An item holds a number of units of each of its materials set by its burden** (see
[IV §1](04-items.md)), doubling at each step:

| Burden | Units of each material |
|---|---|
| Insignificant | 1 |
| Light | 2 |
| Medium | 4 |
| Heavy | 8 |

The figure is the same for every material an item holds: a knife is two units of metal and two of
wood. **Making a thing costs exactly what breaking it down returns**, so the two directions can never
disagree.

**Three materials are refined.** Metal is what ore becomes, cloth what fibre becomes, and leather what
hide becomes, each at a station whose work turns the one into the other (§2). No chain of making is
longer than that single step: a refined material is never refined again.

## 2. Stations

A station is a kind of point of interest — an anvil, a forge hearth, a workbench, a loom, a spinning
wheel, a potter's wheel, a tanning pit, a tallow vat, a cutting table, a leather bench — and **every
station works exactly one material**. What is taken apart there gives back that material and no
other; what is made there is paid for in it. Several stations may work the same material, the loom
and the spinning wheel both working fibre, but no station works two.

| Material | Worked at |
|---|---|
| Ore | the forge hearth |
| Metal | the anvil |
| Wood | the workbench |
| Fibre | the loom, the spinning wheel |
| Cloth | the cutting table |
| Hide | the tanning pit |
| Leather | the leather bench |
| Clay | the potter's wheel |
| Fat | the tallow vat |

Bone and stone are held by many things and worked at no station.

A workshop commonly holds more than one station: a forge has its hearth and its anvil, a weaver's
shop its loom and a board to cut the cloth on.

## 3. Trades

A trade is a modus mentis bearing the **Craft** function. It declares three things and everything
else of it follows:

- **its station**, the one kind it works at, and with it the material it works;
- **its implement**, the one item it works with;
- **what it makes**, from one to five kinds of item.

**No two trades share a station and an implement.** Two trades may work at the anvil, and two may work
with shears, but no two at the anvil with the hammer. It is this that lets the implement in hand say
which trade is at work (§5).

Every trade requires the use of the hands, so that no beast may hold one; its memory is procedural.
A trade bears three functions — Craft, Action and **Will** — and as a will it wants five things, in
this order of preference:

1. to set to work at its station, as itself;
2. to take up something made of its material — gathered, picked up, cut from a carcass, mined, felled
   or dug;
3. to buy something made of its material from one who sells it;
4. to take up its implement;
5. to study, by examination or contemplation, whatever teaches it.

As with every will (see [II §7](02-memory.md#7-what-the-modi-mentis-are-for)), the nearest of these
is pursued, and between wants equally near, the first. The second and third are broad by nature —
wood and fibre lie in most places — and a trade is not the less a desire for being satisfied often.

## 4. Leave

**A station inside a workshop that has a master is used only with the master's leave.** The master
is the workshop's owner of highest authority who is alive and can speak. A station that no one owns —
an anvil among ruins, an anvil set out in a street, a tanning pit in a public quarter — is used by
whoever comes upon it, and no leave is asked or wanted.

The leave is asked in conversation, and only by a body holding a trade whose station stands in that
workshop; no other has a reason to ask, and none is offered the asking. The conversation is a
**matter of consequence** on the ladders of [VII §2](07-social.md). Won, it gives the leave; lost, it
gives nothing, and the asking may be made again.

**Leave once given is given for good.** It covers every station of the workshop, it is remembered of
the location on every later visit, and it makes the body's presence in the workshop no trespass (see
[VI §7](06-narration.md)). It gives no title to what lies on the workshop's shelves: taking from a
master's stock is what it always was.

It is to be observed that the asking is **a step on the way**, never a thing a trade wants. A trade's
will reckons the leave a conversation would give, and so plans through it: ask the master, then set to
work. Being a step and not the end, the conversation is carried by whichever speaking modus mentis is
judged fittest, so that a trade which has no speech of its own is not thereby barred from a master's
workshop.

## 5. Setting to work

Setting to work at a station is an act of **difficulty two**, offered once for every trade the body
holds for that station, and only where the station may be used (§4).

**It requires an implement**, combined with the act as any required implement is (see [IV
§7](04-items.md)). The trade's own implement is accepted without question. Any other is judged as
every substitute is: whether it could do the work of the implement wanted, and whether the hand that
holds it has proficiency enough for so poor a fit. A smith may try his trade with an axe.

**The implement names the trade.** Where it is the implement of one of the body's trades for that
station, that trade is the one at work, whichever was proposed; otherwise the trade proposed is.

Success puts the body to work at the station (§6) and teaches the trade at work. Failure costs one of
three things, drawn as every failure's cost is ([VI §8](06-narration.md)): a scrape upon the hands,
the hour lost, or a point of the phase's budget.

## 6. At the station

Two operations are open, as often as the body likes.

- **Breaking down.** Any carried item holding the station's material may be destroyed, and its units
  of that material are added to what is in hand. **Only the station's material is kept**; whatever else
  the item held is lost with it — a knife broken down at the anvil gives up its metal, and its handle is
  gone. A container must be empty to be broken down.
- **Making.** Any thing the trade makes may be made, at the cost in the station's material of the units
  the made thing holds (§1). It goes into the pack, and is not made where it could not be placed there.

**What is in hand is not kept.** There is no store of material: what remains when the work ends is
thrown away. The quantity a body can bring to a station is therefore bounded by what it can carry
(see [IV](04-items.md)), and the work is best brought to the station whole.

Nothing in the work is rolled, and none of it takes time. The trade's level and the implement's count
once, in setting to work (§5).

## 7. The routine of a station

Setting to work successfully teaches a **Craft** routine (see [IX](09-routines.md)): the station, the
trade, and the implement the act was won with where that was not the trade's own. It is learned
wherever the body may stand — on open ground, or in a workshop whose master has given leave, the leave
being kept for good.

Walked, it sets the body to work at the station directly, with no act rolled. It cannot be walked when
the station is gone; when the trade is no longer held; when the implement is not carried — the
trade's own, or the one it was learned with; when the station may no longer be used; or when an enemy
stands in the area. Closing the work returns the body to the world.
