# IX. Mental Maps and Routines

A **mental map** is what the character remembers of one **location** (one place on the world map, such as a village or a wood). A **routine** is a shortcut into a remembered location: it puts the character straight at a known spot, at a known time of day, doing a known thing.

**In short**

1. The character remembers 4 locations per point of anamnesis score (long-term memory organ, see [Chapter I §6](01-anatomy.md#6-derived-values)). A human at the maximum score of 5 remembers 20.
2. A visit makes that location's map the newest. When memory is full, the oldest unlocked map is forgotten, with every routine learned there.
3. A locked map is never forgotten to make room.
4. A routine is learned by doing something in a location: entering an area, meeting someone, gathering, trading, working or crafting.
5. Walking a routine skips the way in and opens the area (one spot inside a location), the period of the day and the phase (a stretch of play with its own rules, such as a conversation or a barter) it was learned at. It is checked first, and refused if what it needs is gone.
6. No routine is learned on private ground, except a workshop whose master has given lasting leave.

## 1. Mental maps

A mental map holds what the character remembers of one location.

**Rules**

1. A map holds the **areas** (spots inside the location where the character can stand) the character has stood in, the ways it has gone by, what its observations took in from where it stood (see [Chapter VI](06-narration.md)), and the routines learned there.
2. The memory belongs to the character, not to the place. It lasts only as long as the map.
3. **Number of maps = anamnesis score × 4.** This is the Mental Maps value (see [Chapter I §6](01-anatomy.md#6-derived-values)).
4. Maps are kept in order of last visit.
5. **A visit** (one stay in a location, from arrival to leaving) **makes its location's map the newest.** A location already remembered keeps its map and moves to the front.
6. A location not remembered gets a new map. If every map is taken, the **oldest** map is forgotten to make room.
7. A forgotten map takes everything with it: its areas and ways are unknown again, and its routines are gone.
8. A map can be **locked**. A locked map is never forgotten to make room; the oldest unlocked map is forgotten instead.
9. If every map is locked, or the anamnesis allows none, the visit keeps a temporary map. It works like any other while the visit lasts: its walks are recorded and its known ways can be taken again. It is forgotten when the visit ends, routines and all.
10. If the anamnesis score drops, the maps over the new number are forgotten at the next visit, oldest first. Locked maps are kept.
11. Routines have no limit of their own. A map keeps every routine learned in its location, and they are forgotten only with the map.

| Anamnesis score | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Locations remembered | 0 | 4 | 8 | 12 | 16 | 20 |

## 2. The seven kinds of routine

A routine records where the character ended up, when, and why: an **area**, a **period of the day** (one of the parts the day is divided into, see [Chapter V §8](05-travel.md#8-the-periods-of-the-day)), and the **phase** that opens there (a stretch of play with its own rules, see [Chapter VI §3](06-narration.md)).

**Rules**

1. Walking a routine puts the character in that area at that period of the day.
2. It skips every path, door, climb and crossing between the edge of the location and that spot. The way is known and is not walked again.
3. Each routine belongs to one location, one area and the period of the day it was learned in.
4. The same thing learned twice in the same place at the same period is kept once.
5. The same thing learned at another period is a separate routine. Reason: who is present and what is possible change with the time of day.
6. A routine learned again keeps its place in the list, but what it records is updated, such as a merchant's current catalogue.
7. A conversation the character did not start, such as being confronted over something seen, teaches no routine. Reason: nobody goes back to be caught again.

| Kind | Learned when | Walking it opens |
|---|---|---|
| **Go to** | the character walks into an area other than the one its visit started in (every such area, at the period it is reached); also the area a location is left from | narration (the phase of observing, thinking and acting; see [Chapter VI](06-narration.md)), in that area |
| **Meet** | the character opens a conversation with someone | narration in that person's area, with its first observation on them |
| **Gather** | an item is successfully gathered, dug, mined, fished or cut | the gathering stay (§5) |
| **Buy** | a merchant agrees to sell | the barter, buying, with no conversation first; the character returns to the world map when it closes |
| **Sell** | a merchant agrees to buy | the barter, selling, with no conversation first; the character returns to the world map when it closes |
| **Work** | a master agrees to take the character on | the stint of work, with no conversation first |
| **Craft** | the character successfully sets to work at a station (a fixed workplace such as a forge; see [Chapter X](10-crafting.md)) | the work at that station, with no act rolled; the character returns to the world map when it closes |

## 3. Where no routine is learned

Some places and things never teach a routine.

**Rules**

1. **No routine is learned on private ground** (an area where the character has no business; standing there is a trespass, see [Chapter VI §7](06-narration.md)). Reason: a routine there would be a free way back in, without the risk.
2. Private ground teaches no routine even when its householder has let the character stay ([Chapter VI §7](06-narration.md)). That leave lasts only the visit, and a routine is walked on a later visit, where no one has given it.
3. Exception: a workshop whose master has given the character use of it ([Chapter X §4](10-crafting.md)). That leave is permanent, so the ground stays open on every later visit too.
4. Working a station without its master's leave teaches no routine. Reason: the work is a crime and cannot be repeated by walking a routine.
5. A routine whose area has since become private cannot be walked.
6. Nothing created during a visit teaches a routine. A corpse, the wreck of broken furniture, and anything either holds are absent from the next visit, so gathering from them teaches nothing.

## 4. When a routine can be walked

Before a routine can be chosen, the location is rebuilt as it now is and the routine is checked against it. It is checked again on arrival.

**Rules**

1. In every case the area must still exist and must still be open ground (not private).
2. Each kind of routine also has its own check, in the table below.
3. Companions are not checked, nor anything done on the way the first time. The way is known.
4. The only thing a routine requires the character to bring is **the implement it was learned with** (a tool such as a pick or a hammer). A seam learned with a pick is not worked without one, nor an anvil without a hammer.
5. The implement is required, never used up.
6. A routine found unwalkable on arrival is dropped, and the visit goes on as an ordinary arrival.
7. Walking a routine is a visit like any other, and makes its location's map the newest.

| Kind | Cannot be walked when |
|---|---|
| Go to | no further check |
| Meet | the person is gone for good, is elsewhere at that period of the day, or counts the character an enemy |
| Buy, Sell | any Meet condition; or the area is not the merchant's premises; or the merchant would no longer trade (see [Chapter VII](07-social.md)); or an enemy is in the area |
| Work | any Meet condition; or the work is no longer offered; or the master would not take the character on; or an enemy is in the area |
| Gather | the source is gone; or the item no longer grows there at all; or the character's anatomy cannot perform that way of taking it; or the implement it was learned with is not carried; or an enemy is in the area |
| Craft | the station is gone; or the trade is no longer held; or its implement, or the one it was learned with, is not carried; or the station may no longer be used; or an enemy is in the area |

## 5. The gathering stay

A **gathering stay** is the phase a Gather routine opens: the character stays at the source for a chosen number of days and collects what grows.

**Rules**

1. Only a Gather routine opens a gathering stay.
2. The stay lasts a number of days chosen before it starts, from 1 to 360.
3. **The source has slots, and each slot regrows.** They are the same item slots that are picked by hand in narration.
4. A slot that is taken stays empty for 9 days where the item grows wild, and for 36 days where it is kept or cultivated.
5. A slot taken by hand is empty for a stay too, and a slot taken during a stay is empty for the hand.
6. **Each slot gives its item** as soon as it is full, and again each time it regrows during the stay.
7. **Nothing is rolled.** The gathering was learned by doing it once; the routine is knowing how.
8. So the yield depends only on the length of the stay. A short stay takes what is there now. Each further regrowth the days allow adds one more of each slot's item.
9. **Nothing is taken that cannot be carried.** Once the character cannot hold another item, the stay runs to its end but nothing more is taken. The slots not reached are left as they were.
10. The clock moves forward by the whole stay.
11. When the stay ends the character returns to the world map, not to the location. Reason: the scene it arrived in is long out of date.

Example: a wild bush has 2 full slots. A stay of 20 days takes 2 items on the first day. Each slot regrows on day 9 and day 18, so the stay yields 6 items in all, if they can be carried.

## Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mental map | What the character remembers of one location: areas, ways, observations and routines |
| Locked map | A map that is never forgotten to make room |
| Anamnesis | The organ of long-term memory; its score sets how many maps are kept |
| Routine | A shortcut into a remembered location: an area, a period of the day and a phase |
| Location | One place on the world map |
| Visit | One stay in a location, from arrival to leaving |
| Area | One spot inside a location |
| Period of the day | One of the parts the day is divided into |
| Phase | One stretch of play with its own rules, such as narration, a conversation or a barter |
| Narration | The phase of observing, thinking and acting in a scene |
| Private ground | An area where the character has no business; standing there is a trespass |
| Station | A fixed workplace used for making things |
| Implement | An item used as a tool, such as a pick or a hammer |
| Slot | One item a gathering source holds; it regrows after it is taken |
| Gathering stay | The phase a Gather routine opens: days spent collecting at a source |
