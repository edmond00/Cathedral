# VIII. Of Combat

Combat is fought upon a grid of cells, in turns, with a fixed budget of motion per turn. It is the
one mode of play in which the body is spent directly rather than through a modus mentis.

## 1. Order and budget

**The order of turns** is settled once, at the outset: each fighter adds the **ears'** measure of
initiative to a single die, and the order follows the totals.

**The budget of a turn** is the **cinetic points**: the **backbone's** score doubled, never below
one. Cinetic points pay for movement and for every skill used. A turn ends when they are spent, or
when it is ended deliberately.

**Movement** costs one cinetic point per span, and a span is the **legs'** measure — one cell at the
weakest, then two, four, six, eight.

## 2. The ground

Six kinds of cell:

| Kind | Effect |
|---|---|
| Free space | Passable. |
| Hard obstacle | Impassable, save by a skill of leaping. |
| Soft obstacle | Impedes. |
| Treacherous terrain | May cost the footing, and with it the turn. |
| Dangerous terrain | As above, and worse. |
| Exit | The way out of the fight. |

The **feet's** measure of equilibrium resists the loss of footing. A fighter who slips loses the
turn.

**Adjacency is eight-way, and this holds for blows as well as for steps.** Range is measured as a
straight line — so a bow's reach stays circular — *and* the eight surrounding cells always count
regardless. Without that exception a diagonal neighbour would stand at a distance of one and
two-fifths, outside the reach of a hand, and two fighters could stand shoulder to shoulder unable to
strike one another.

## 3. Mediums

A **medium** is the physical thing a blow is struck with. Three kinds:

- **An organ** — the hands, the feet, the fangs, the claws.
- **A body region** — the upper limbs entire, and the like. Its level is the region's total score.
- **A weapon** — held in a hand. Its level is the weapon's own.

Where an organ's parts act **independently**, each part is a medium in its own right and strikes
separately, drawing dice from its own score alone: the left hand and the right hand are two mediums,
and a blow may be thrown once with each in a single turn. Where the parts act together, the whole
organ is one medium, acting once per turn, at the sum of its parts. The hands, the feet and the arms
are of the first kind; the legs, which one runs and dodges with together, of the second.

A single-part organ is always a whole-organ medium.

## 4. Fighting skills

A fighting skill is usable when two conditions hold: the fighter **holds the modus mentis** that
unlocks it, and the **medium** it requires is present and not disabled.

Each skill names a **primary modus mentis** and may name **secondary** ones; the levels of every
named modus mentis the fighter holds are summed. Its dice are then

> base dice + (medium level × its multiplier) + (summed modus mentis level × its multiplier)

Most skills declare no base dice and derive everything from the two levels.

A skill costs cinetic points, and may cost vital heat besides.

**Six kinds of effect:**

| Effect | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Attack** | Wounds, if the dice carry. |
| **Defence posture** | Raises the defence pool until the fighter's next turn. |
| **Defence** | A reactive guard — adds defence dice to one or all incoming blows. |
| **Special movement** | Permits a passage otherwise closed, such as leaping a hard obstacle. |
| **Utility** | Everything else. |
| **Buff** | Applies a state to the user. No target, no dice, no wound. |

### Of buffs

A buff has nothing to strike and nothing to resist it, so it **rolls nothing**. It costs **vital
heat**, and the cost is

> 10 − the skill's total level, confined to the range 1–10

which runs backwards from every other formula in this manual: **level makes a buff cheaper, not
stronger.** Its whole effect is the state it applies.

### Of guards

Defence is a **number of dice**, so guards are bought rather than rolled. A parry, a dodge, a cover,
a defensive stance each cost cinetic points and immediately add dice to the defence pool for the
turn.

**No self-targeted roll can ever produce a wound.** The one self-targeted skill that still rolls is
the feint — someone must be convinced — and its sixes become bonus attack dice rather than injury.

## 5. The exchange

A blow is settled by comparing two counts of sixes.

**The attack pool** is the skill's dice, as §4 computes them, plus the **genitories'** measure of
natural attack, plus whatever bonuses stand.

**The defence pool** is:

| Contribution | From |
|---|---|
| Natural defence | The **lower limbs**, at one die per three points |
| Guards and stances | Whatever defensive skills are in effect |
| Armour | The defence dice of every worn garment covering the section struck |

**The attacker wins when their sixes exceed the defender's.** Ties go to the defender.

Because armour is charged against the **section** struck, the place of the blow must be known before
the dice are cast. An unaimed attack therefore **pre-rolls its location**, weighted by each part's
share of the pool of wounds it could take, so that a large region is likelier than a small organ.
Where a skill names its own location, or the attacker chooses one, that stands instead.

A blow that lands **wounds**, drawn from the pool the location allows. There is no roll afterwards
that softens it: severity is not mitigated after the fact. Resilience is expressed over the long run
instead, through the **viscera's** measure of healing.

A blow that lands also **teaches**: every modus mentis contributing to the skill gains a point of
experience, save those already at their ceiling.

## 6. States

A landed blow may apply a **status** to the target. States are of three lifetimes, and the
distinction matters:

- **For this turn** — expiring at the **end** of the bearer's own turn. It must be so: the beginning
  of a turn comes round only after every other fighter has acted, which would stretch a one-turn
  state into a full round.
- **For the round** — a state that fires while others attack expires nowhere else.
- **For the fight** — some states never expire at all.

Certain states **stack** rather than duplicating: a second bleeding deepens the first.

## 7. Falling, learning, and flight

**Knockdown.** A fighter on the ground rolls the **pineal gland's** measure in dice at the start of
their turn and needs one six to rise.

**Learning under arms.** An unknown fighting skill may be attempted in the midst of a fight, with
the **cerebellum's** measure in dice.

**Flight.** Leaving the fight requires the **feet's** measure in dice and one six among them.

Walking away ends the fight and nothing else. Enmity persists — the person who drew steel on you is
still an enemy on your next visit, still an armed presence in the room, and any failed act before
them starts it again.

## 8. Vital heat in combat

Every cost in heat is paid the same way: humors are consumed in rotation from the four queues until
the charge is met (see [Chapter III §3](03-humors.md#3-vital-heat)). Combat is therefore, over its
length, a drain upon the same reserve that pays for travel — and a body that fights its way across a
country may find it cannot cross the next stretch of it.
