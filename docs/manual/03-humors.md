# III. Of the Humors

The humors are at once the body's fuel and its instrument of fortune. They are held in four queues,
rendered by four organs, spent to sustain exertion, and expended one at a time to bend a die that
has already fallen.

## 1. The four queues

Each of the four **sanguific** organs — the *hepar*, the *paunch*, the *pulmones* and the *spleen* —
maintains a queue of its own, of exactly **forty-nine** places. A queue is a strict procession:

- the **front** is where a newly rendered humor arrives;
- the **back** is the exit, and holds the oldest humor in the queue.

The queue is always full. Nothing is ever added without something leaving.

**Secretion** is the ordinary motion: a humor is rendered at the front, everything advances by one
place, and the humor at the back departs. **Consumption** is the reverse reading of the same motion:
the humor at the back is taken and spent, and the organ renders a fresh one at the front to replace
it.

## 2. What an organ renders

Which humor an organ renders is decided by that organ's score alone, and the four proportions always
sum to a hundred.

| Organ score | Blood | Yellow bile | Black bile | Phlegm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 % | 40 % | 50 % | 10 % |
| 1 | 12 % | 35 % | 42 % | 11 % |
| 2 | 25 % | 30 % | 33 % | 12 % |
| 3 | 37 % | 25 % | 25 % | 13 % |
| 4 | 49 % | 20 % | 17 % | 14 % |

The human sanguific organs reach three; certain bestial species reach four.

The doctrine is legible in the table: **a sound organ renders chiefly blood; a failing one renders
chiefly black bile.** Phlegm is nearly indifferent to the organ's condition. Whence the four
temperaments — the hepar inclining to the sanguine in health and the bilious as it is impaired, the
spleen the seat of the melancholic, and so forth — are not separate doctrines but one table read at
four different scores.

## 3. Vital heat

Every humor carries a quantity of **vital heat**, positive or negative, from **−5** to **+3**. Heat
is what pays for exertion: the crossing of the world's cells, and certain acts in combat.

A debt of heat is paid by consuming humors **in rotation** — hepar, paunch, pulmones, spleen, and
round again — one at a time until the debt is met. A queue that has gone critical (§5) is passed
over. A humor of negative heat consumed against a debt does not pay it but deepens it.

The cardinal humors are moderate: blood carries +1, phlegm and yellow bile nothing, black bile
nothing. The extremes belong to the further humors — the products of ingestion, of intoxication, of
pestilence and of the passions — which run from a venom at −5 to the richest at +3. A body may
therefore be well fuelled or poisoned by what it has taken in, and the queue records that history
for forty-nine places.

**When every queue is critical, exertion can no longer be paid for, and the body dies of
starvation.** This is the one way the humoral system kills outright.

## 4. The transmuting virtues

Every humor save black bile also carries a **transmuting virtue**: a licence to alter one die of a
roll that has already been cast but not yet accepted.

Four forms of virtue exist:

| Form | Written | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Null** | N → N | Applies to any die and changes none of them. |
| **Numeric** | N → N ± X | Shifts any die by a fixed amount, confined to the range 1–6. |
| **Conversion** | S → T, or N → T | Turns a die showing S into T. A wildcard form applies to any face. |
| **Reroll** | S → ?, or N → ? | Casts a die showing S afresh. A wildcard form applies to any face. |

Blood adds one; yellow bile subtracts one; phlegm is null. The further humors carry the conversions
and rerolls, some of them ruinous — a humor that turns every six into a one is as real as one that
turns any face into a six.

**Only the humor at the back of a queue may be spent.** Four humors are therefore available at any
roll, one per organ, and which four they are is the accumulated consequence of everything the body
has secreted and consumed since it was made.

**Spending is not consumption.** A humor spent as a modifier is removed from the back and *no
replacement is rendered*; the queue simply advances, and whatever stood behind the spent humor
becomes the new usable one. This is why the null virtue is worth having: it costs a place in the
budget and moves no die, but it clears the way to whatever lies behind it. Without it, a phlegm at
the back would wall off the whole organ for that roll.

The number of virtues that may be applied to a single roll is the **viscera's** measure — the organ
score, never below one.

## 5. Black bile

Black bile is the exception to every rule above, and the queues are shaped around it.

It carries **no transmuting virtue** and **no vital heat**, so it can neither bend a die nor pay for
anything. And it **cannot be shed by ordinary motion**: when something must leave the back of a
queue, the removal falls instead on the **rearmost humor that is not black bile**, and the black
bile behind it shifts inward to close the gap.

Black bile therefore accumulates at the back of a queue and stays there, and every accumulation
narrows what remains. A queue entirely composed of black bile is **critical**: nothing further can
be inserted into it, nothing can be drawn from it, and it is passed over in the rotation of
consumption. All four critical is death.

## 6. How the queues are first filled

A protagonist's queues are filled at the close of composition, by secretion from the final scores of
the four sanguific organs, with **black bile withheld** — its share of the distribution being
redistributed across blood, phlegm and yellow bile. A protagonist therefore begins with no black
bile at all, whatever their organs.

A fraction of the places equal to the **youthfulness** measure — the heart's score in tens, up to
half — is then overwritten with the humor of youth, which carries +2 heat and adds two to a die. A
warm heart is thus a fuller reserve and a luckier one, for a while.

Other characters are filled otherwise: a queue of blood, secreted upon ten times. They begin nearly
pure and grow more characteristic of their organs as they are drawn upon.

## 7. What moves a queue

The queues are not idle. They advance whenever:

- **exertion is paid for** — a cell of the world crossed, a skill used in combat that names a cost
  in heat, a buff sustained;
- **a virtue is spent** upon a die;
- **something is ingested**, which pushes its whole composition into the queue of the organ that
  receives it — the paunch, the hepar or the pulmones, according to how it was taken (see [Chapter
  IV §6](04-items.md#6-consumables));
- **an event of consequence occurs**, which may push a humor of the passions into the spleen.

Everything that enters displaces something at the far end, and everything spent leaves a gap that
the organ fills according to its condition. The state of a body's humors an hour hence is therefore
a function of what it did and what its organs are — which is the whole of the doctrine.
