Proscribed Palimpsest · branch multi-target-wills

Wills with several targets

A will used to want one kind of act and refuse everything else. Now each will wants one to five things, the planner pursues the nearest, and 41 specific crafts and lores joined the wills. Measured headless with --will-audit: every location factory the audits sweep, three location ids (one for a town or city), morning and night, an actor carrying nothing.

Related coverage is the share of starts, from an area of a kind the will declares it belongs in, from which the planner finds a way to something it wants. It is the playtest complaint measured. Wills at 60% or more in their own places: 62 of 114 before, 88 of 153 after. The audit fails under 60% overall, and fails any will with several targets that reaches more than 75% of location kinds.

The 'before' column is the code at the baseline commit, with the place declarations added as data and behaviour untouched. Two existing wills had their places re-declared after the baseline (tea_lore to the tea garden's own areas, hop_lore adding taverns); every other existing will is measured against the places it declared before any change.

Overall related coverage stays level while the number of measured wills grows by 41: the original wills improve, and the new ones, mostly trades measured against the rooms their trade is plied in, sit lower because an empty-handed body has nothing to buy with in a fresh scene, and some trades' goods are not in every room of their kind.

Generating NPC skills now takes one draw per pool from the person's generator, so changing the catalogue reshuffles skills only. This branch's conversions therefore build different people than before at every location id, once.

Content added

  • Wild growth: a mushroom cluster on 45% of woodland and damp areas, and a herb patch (Woodland Herbs: valerian, wild mint; Meadow Herbs: wild thyme, chamomile, meadowsweet) on 40% of woodland and temperate meadow areas, where the area holds none — SceneFactory.PlaceWildGrowth, drawn from a generator of its own, off in the test location.
  • An axe bitten into a chopping block in every woodshed (the woodcutter's and charcoal burner's camp).
  • A spare pick in every cave miner's ore store.
  • A shovel against the churchyard wall in every village with a church walk.
  • A shovel and a pruning hook on the barn's tool rack of every arable holding.
  • Key pegs with a ring of keys in the gatehouse of every edifice (castle, fortress, temple, monastery, school, palace…).
  • A bolt of sailcloth and a bale of oakum at the hull on the slip in every port shipyard.
  • A ring of keys is now a tool made for unlock_door, and the planner's tool detour looks for any item made for a verb as well as its reference tools.
  • Tidewatching granted beside hard labour by the childhood fragment of the docks — the one path of the childhood that left no general thinker (rule C3).
  • ItemKind: 50 kinds tagged across all 500 items (every item carries at least one); a will narrows by kind with YieldingKind and Selling.

Refusals in play

A fixed route, run on the baseline build and on this one with tools/wills/refusals.sh: forest, plain, village, city and mushroom cellar, location ids 1–3, morning, --playground --seed 42, eight wills held that exist in both builds. Each run reads every will's plan on arrival, then has three of the wills think about the first thing the opening shows.

Idle means nothing it wants can be done anywhere in the place, so it refuses everything there. Of the 90 thinkings asked of a will about an arbitrary object, 82 refused and 8 proposed a step before; 79 refused and 11 proposed after. That second figure moves little because a will refuses an object that is not on its way, whatever its targets; the route asks about whatever the opening shows, not about what the will would attend to. The counts are deterministic for a given build (fixed seed and location ids), but the people of every location differ between the two builds (see the note on NPC draws).

What got worse

Original wills whose related coverage fell by more than two points:

willrelated beforerelated afterbreadth beforebreadth aftertargets now
brawling100%19%100%29%AttackVerb on somebody hostile ; ProposeToBuyVerb selling Weapon

Brawling falls from 100% to about 19% in taverns and streets: it now wants somebody hostile, or a weapon to buy. Before, it planned to strike any passer-by, which the click then refused in front of witnesses; the plan is honest now, and a brawler in a peaceful alehouse has nothing to do there.

Lockpicking and jailcraft remain near 0% in houses and strongholds: a pick for a lock is carried by people, not left in rooms, and a locked door's keys hang at an edifice's gatehouse, which a house does not have.
